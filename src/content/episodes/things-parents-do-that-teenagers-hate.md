What's the one thing your parents do that drives you crazy?

That's the actual question WAG throws out at the end of this episode — and honestly, by that point they've already answered it about twenty different ways themselves.

In this episode of the [WAG Podcast](/), Angelina, Scarlett, and Annabella — the real teen hosts behind the show, [meet them here](/hosts/) — go story by story through the cringey, annoying, and genuinely hilarious things their own parents have done to them. Not a generic listicle. Their actual grounding history, actual lies their parents tell, and actual arguments that are apparently still ongoing at home.

Here's the real breakdown.

## The Grounding Hall of Fame

Some of these still don't feel fair, and the girls know it.

Getting grounded for a fifteen-minute shower makes the list. So does getting grounded for not shutting a door all the way. Add in a full-on "Grounding Games" bit that traces back to a church trip, and a running 6:30 PM phone curfew that none of them are happy about, and you've basically got a teenager's entire case against parental discipline in one episode.

It's not that the punishments were dramatic. It's that they were over things that, from a teenager's side of the room, genuinely did not seem worth it.

## Parents Trying to Be Cool (It Does Not Work)

Nothing gets a bigger reaction than a parent attempting current slang.

The episode dedicates real time to parents trying to use Gen Z lingo — and getting it wrong in the specific way only a parent can. There's a whole tangent about the difference between "spilling the juice" and "spilling the tea," which somehow becomes its own bit. And then there's the classic parent trap question, "Do I look old?" — a question the girls agree has no correct answer.

There's also the Korean face mask jump scare, which is exactly what it sounds like: a parent, mid-skincare-routine, becoming genuinely terrifying to walk in on.

## The Excuses (and the Lies)

This is where it gets a little more pointed.

One dad gets called out directly for what the episode frames as gaslighting — specifically around yard work. There's also "the light in the car" lie parents tell, and the very specific betrayal of a parent "forgetting" they already said yes to something.

And then there's the line that comes up whenever a teenager gets blamed for anything that goes wrong: "It's because of your phone."

## Strict Parents, Dating, and Everything Else

The episode doesn't stop at grounding stories. It moves into strict parents and dating, the ongoing struggle of finding a prom dress that actually clears a church dress code, and parents who brag about their kids to literally anyone who'll listen — which the girls admit is somehow both embarrassing and kind of nice.

There's also a real, unresolved debate: do parents love pets more than they love their own kids? Nobody fully wins that one.

## So... What Do Parents Actually Do That Teenagers Hate?

Pull it all together and the pattern isn't really about punishment. It's about the small, specific, weirdly universal stuff — the wake-up call that isn't gentle, the slang that isn't landing, the door that "should" have been shut all the way.

None of it is invented for the internet. It's just what actually happens in a house with teenagers in it.
