# WAG Title Formula Library v1

*A creative-meeting reference for turning validated video formats into title constructions. Every formula and example below is pulled directly from the verified format write-ups in [WAG_FORMAT_LIBRARY.md](WAG_FORMAT_LIBRARY.md) (real titles, real view counts, real WAG adaptations already vetted in that document) — nothing here is invented for this library. Categories are grouped by evidenced formula overlap, not by the original format names, because the underlying research showed some formats sharing one title chassis (most notably Investigation and Testing/Review, which the source research calls "the same claim-test-resolve chassis... legend-testing is a subtype of it, not a different format").*

**Categories included:** Challenge/Commitment, Investigation & Verification Testing, Completion ("Every X"), Competition/Head-to-Head, Transformation.

**Category deliberately excluded:** "Curiosity" as a standalone bucket — every format names a curiosity driver, but none evidences a distinct *title construction* for curiosity separate from the five buckets below. Forcing a sixth category here would mean re-labeling formulas already covered, not adding real new evidence.

---

## 1. Challenge / Commitment

*Merges Commitment + Confinement/Trapped — both rest on the identical mechanism (one stated rule + a hard finish line, tension supplied by time), just applied to open movement (Commitment) vs. a bounded space (Confinement). MrBeast's Circle example appears under both formats for exactly this reason.*

### Formula 1.1 — "I [Verb]ED On/For [absurdly limited resource] For [stated duration]"
**Real example:** Ryan Trahan, *"I Survived On $0.01 For 30 Days"* (30M views)
**WAG applications:** *"We Only Ate 1-Star Texas Restaurants For 7 Days Straight"* (extends WAG's own #1 post-pivot performer into a week-long commitment) / *"My Sister Made Every Decision For Me For 7 Days"* (duration-extension of WAG's real prior art, "My Sister Decides What We Eat for 24 Hours")

### Formula 1.2 — "[Stated duration] [Committed/Trapped state] [Location/condition]"
**Real examples:** MrBeast, *"7 Days Stranded At Sea"* (437M) / *"I Spent 7 Days In Solitary Confinement"* (298M) and *"I Spent 50 Hours In Solitary Confinement"* (344M) — same chassis run as a real two-entry franchise at two durations
**WAG applications:** *"We Weren't Allowed To Leave the Property Until Sunrise"* (literal-boundary variant of WAG's proven Overnight Cryptid bucket) / *"How Long Can 3 Girls Survive Locked In a Storm Shelter?"* (a real, regionally normal Texas structure)

### Formula 1.3 — "How Many/Long Can (I Secretly) Live In [ordinary bounded public place]?"
**Real example:** Airrack, *"How Many Days Can I Secretly Live In a Grocery Store?"* (21M — franchise also run on a mall, 17M, and a 7-Eleven, 5.3M, see Counterexamples)
**WAG applications:** Not directly portable as written — the real mechanism (unsupervised covert access to someone else's operating business) is a legal/access risk WAG doesn't have and shouldn't attempt. The transferable piece (bounded-space + detection-clock) is better captured for WAG by Formula 1.4.

### Formula 1.4 — "[We Got] Locked In/Handcuffed [bounded space/each other] for [duration]"
**Real example:** MrBeast, *"Last To Leave Circle Wins $500,000"* (561M — franchise repeated at "...Wins $10,000," 99M, and "...Their Circle Wins $500,000," 218M)
**WAG applications:** *"We Got Locked In Our Garage for 24 Hours (No Phones)"* / *"Handcuffed Together Until We Escape the Room"* (bounded, timed variant of WAG's real prior art, "Handcuffed to My Sister for 24 Hours!")

## 2. Investigation & Verification Testing

*Merges Verification/Investigation + Testing/Review — the research's own conclusion is that these are one chassis (claim -> test -> resolve), just pointed at legends vs. reviews.*

### Formula 2.1 — "[Duration] in/at [Superlative] [Place or Legend Name]! ([Payoff tease])"
**Real examples:** WAG, *"24 Hours in America's Most Haunted Hotel! \*Ghost in Room\*"* (171K) / WAG, *"24 HOURS in the GOATMAN'S FOREST! (It Followed Us!)"* (11K — see Counterexamples) / FaZe Rug, *"Our Terrifying Night at World's Most Haunted Museum."* (9M)
**WAG applications:** *"24 Hours Testing the Chupacabra Legend! (Is It Really Out There?)"* (a real, widely-reported South/Central Texas cryptid, structurally new vs. the two already-produced entries) / *"We Tried to See the Bragg Road Ghost Light! (Real or Not?)"* (a real, long-documented Texas phenomenon, forces a stakeout method instead of equipment-in-a-room)

### Formula 2.2 — "I Tested [Negative-Rating Label] [Category]"
**Real examples:** Ryan Trahan, *"I Tested 1-Star Restaurants"* (35.5M) / *"I Tested 1-Star Hotels (again)"* (45M — franchise also confirmed running "1-Star Drive Thrus" and "1-Star Theme Parks")
**WAG applications:** *"We Tested Every 1-Star Rated Nail Salon In Our City!"* (cheap, fast, low liability) / *"We Tried Every 1-Star Rated Thrift Store In Our City!"* (a teen-relevant category neither Trahan's corpus nor WAG's catalog has used)

### Formula 2.3 — "I [Verb]ED at EVERY WORST-Rated [Category] In [Location]"
**Real example:** WAG, *"I Ate at EVERY WORST Rated Restaurant In a New City!"* (460K — WAG's #1 post-pivot performer; stacks a completion claim on top of a verification claim, likely why it's WAG's strongest entry in either category)
**WAG applications:** *"We Stayed at Texas' Worst-Rated Overnight Rental for 24 Hours!"* (merges with WAG's proven Overnight Cryptid bucket) / *"We Booked Texas' Worst-Rated Haunted House and Fact-Checked Every Complaint!"* (combines verification with natural Halloween-season timing)

## 3. Completion ("Every X")

### Formula 3.1 — "I [verb-past] EVERY [category] [In/At bounded place]!"
**Real examples:** Nick DiGiovanni, *"I Ate School Lunch From Every Country"* (68M) / Airrack, *"I Tried Food From Every Country"* (~3M) / WAG, *"I Ate EVERY Viral TikTok Food In a New City!"* (375K)
**WAG applications:** *"I Tried EVERY Drink On [a real local Texas boba/coffee chain]'s Menu In One Visit!"* / *"I Ate EVERY Item On the Menu At [a specific already-verified WORST-rated restaurant from WAG's own catalog]!"* (lowest-risk — a sequel to a proven winner)

### Formula 3.2 — "We [verb-past] EVERY [category] in [time constraint]"
**Real example:** SIS vs BRO, *"We Tried EVERY Fast Food in 24 hours"* (3.7M)
**WAG applications:** *"We Ate EVERY Food Truck At [a specific real, recurring Texas food truck park] In One Day!"* / *"We Tried EVERY Flavor At [a real regional Texas snack/candy store] Before Time Runs Out!"*

### Formula 3.3 — "We Ranked EVERY [superlative] [category] Within [bound]" *(completion + tier-list resolution)*
**Real example:** Nick DiGiovanni's tier-list resolution mechanic layered onto Formula 3.1 (68M) — the ranking device, not a separate title, is the real evidence here.
**WAG applications:** *"We Ranked EVERY 1-Star Restaurant Within [X] Miles of [their real town]!"* (combines WAG's proven Worst-Rated bucket with a fully Google-Maps-verifiable, un-fakeable claim)

## 4. Competition / Head-to-Head

*Single-creator evidence only — Norris Nuts and SIS vs BRO were both checked and rejected as real examples of this mechanism (they resolve on arbitrary game outcomes or category-sweep completion, not a true head-to-head). Every example below is from Jordan Matter, flagged Medium-High rather than High confidence for that reason.*

### Formula 4.1 — "[Group A] vs [Group B] - Who's [Better/Best] [At X]?"
**Real examples:** Jordan Matter, *"Boys vs Girls - Who's The Better Parent?"* (34M) / *"BEST FRIENDS vs COUPLE: Who's a Better Valentine?"* (37M)
**WAG applications:** *"Girls vs Dad: Who's The Better Parent (For a Day)?"* (sidesteps sister-vs-sister framing entirely) / *"WAG vs. [a real, brand-safe local team] — Who Actually Knows Texas Better?"* (a real outside group, possible sponsor tie-in)

### Formula 4.2 — "Ages [N]-[N] Compete in [Superlative] [Category] Competition"
**Real example:** Jordan Matter, *"Ages 1-50 Compete in EXTREME STRENGTH Competition"* (12M, still climbing at time of check)
**WAG applications:** *"Ages 16-60 Compete in EXTREME [Category] Competition"* (real extended family, zero outside recruiting)

## 5. Transformation

*Confidence: Medium — real but a thinner evidence base than the other four categories; no minute-by-minute transcript verification available for any of the three source examples.*

### Formula 5.1 — "[Person]'s Surprise [OBJECT] MAKEOVER"
**Real example:** Jordan Matter, *"My Daughter's Surprise ROOM MAKEOVER"* (29M)
**WAG applications:** *"We Surprised Annabella With Her Dream Room Makeover!"* (Angelina and Scarlett secretly redo Annabella's actual bedroom in one real day)

### Formula 5.2 — "[Same-day distraction activity] ft/ [epic] [description] [object] makeover"
**Real example:** Jordan Matter, *"Eating & Buying Everything in One Color ft/ epic pink room makeover"* (48M — the highest-viewed of the three Transformation examples; a real correlation with stacking a distraction hook, not a proven cause, since sponsor push/thumbnail/seasonal timing are competing explanations that can't be ruled out)
**WAG applications:** *"We Tested Every Viral [X] While Secretly Building Scarlett's Dream Closet"* (stacks WAG's own proven Viral-Testing engine as the distraction layer)

### Formula 5.3 — "I [Built/verb-past] [big round number] [object] And Gave Them Away!"
**Real example:** MrBeast & Beast Philanthropy, *"I Built 100 Houses And Gave Them Away!"* (271M — also drew real, documented public backlash calling the project performative; Newsweek/Unilad coverage)
**WAG applications:** None recommended. This formula's budget (multi-country giveaway, nonprofit logistics) is incompatible with WAG's no-travel, no-giveaway-budget constraints, and the backlash risk is a real caution against ever framing a WAG transformation as "helping" someone unless WAG genuinely has the resources to back the claim. Listed for completeness of the evidence, not as an actionable WAG formula.

## Counterexamples — title constructions that looked promising but underperformed

*A working title formula is not a guarantee. All three entries use a formula proven to work elsewhere (or even in the same franchise), but the specific video underperformed — cited from the source research, not inferred.*

1. **WAG's own "EVERY Viral TikTok Product In a New City" (x2, ~1.7K views each)** vs. its formula-sibling "I Ate EVERY Viral TikTok Food In a New City!" (375K) and "I Ate at EVERY WORST Rated Restaurant In a New City!" (460K). Same Completion formula (3.1), same channel, same rough production cost — the two flops swapped "food" for "product" with no verification/stakes wrapper. **A bare completion claim, without a real clock, a checkable premise, or a visible-checklist thumbnail, doesn't hold attention** — proof the formula is a container, not a hook by itself.

2. **WAG's own "24 HOURS in the GOATMAN'S FOREST! (It Followed Us!)" (11K views)** vs. its formula-sibling "24 Hours in America's Most Haunted Hotel! \*Ghost in Room\*" (171K). Identical Formula 2.1 chassis, same channel, four months apart — a real 15x drop swapping legends. Named as **the single most important WAG-specific data point** in the whole investigation research; flagged as an unresolved diagnostic question (why did Goatman underperform Hotel?) rather than smoothed over.

3. **Airrack's "How Many Days Can I Secretly Live In a 7-Eleven?" (5.3M views, flagged as underperformer)** vs. its formula-siblings on a grocery store (21M) and a mall (17M). Same Formula 1.3 chassis, same creator, same franchise — the smaller/simpler venue produced meaningfully lower results, suggesting the formula's pull is partly tied to the scale/novelty of the bounded space chosen, not the title construction alone.

---

**Methodology note:** every formula above traces to a specific, cited real title with a real, independently-verified view count — no title pattern was reverse-engineered from a hunch. Where a category's evidence was thin (Transformation) or single-creator (Competition), that's stated in the category header rather than smoothed over, consistent with [[wag_source_verification_standard]]. Any new WAG title built from these formulas still has to pass the existing content-authenticity check ([[wag_content_authenticity_rule]]) — the formula only earns the video's premise if WAG genuinely does the thing, not the other way around.

## Data-model note (WAG Brain)

A `TitleFormula` entity (Pattern, Category, SourceExamples[], Confidence) with child `Example` records (real, evidence-tagged) and child `WagApplication` records (hypothesis until filmed). Counterexamples are their own tagged relationship (`Formula.counterexamples[]`), not a separate table — the point is they attach to the same formula they superficially fit, so a future agent querying "formulas that work" surfaces the failure case in the same breath as the success case, instead of it living in a separate, easy-to-miss list.
