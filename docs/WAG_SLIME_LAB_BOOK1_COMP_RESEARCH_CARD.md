## Synthesis — why these 10 form the right research shelf, and how the Humanity Gate governs the whole exercise

None of these 10 books are being studied so WAG can write like them. Each one was picked because it demonstrates exactly one real market mechanism a generic "write me a kids' slime activity book" AI prompt would not reliably reproduce, or would get wrong by default:

- **The professional floor** (#1 Book of Slime, #5 Weird But True 10): what page density, price point, and non-classroom tone a book in this category has to clear to not read as amateur or as school.
- **The packaging/pricing models** (#2 kit+book, #3 spiral-bound completeness pending re-verification, #9 plush+book bundle): what it actually costs and how it's structured when a physical companion object is involved — directly informs whether Wobbie ships as a mascot-in-print only or eventually as a real sewn product.
- **The psychological mechanisms** (#4 fact-as-spectacle, #6 tactile/artifact page-turn anticipation, #10 optional-not-required digital layer): the *why it works* behind reader engagement, independent of subject matter — consistent with WAG's own standing "mechanisms not genres" research discipline.
- **The craft conventions** (#7 non-condescending safety notes, #8 reluctant-reader pacing): specific, checkable execution details that make the difference between a book that feels made-with-care and one that feels generic.

This is the same evidence discipline WAG applies everywhere else: these comps raise the **floor** — the professional craft bar a physical retail product has to clear (density, safety-note tone, packaging completeness, tactile-mechanic execution, pacing for a struggling reader) — but none of them can supply the **ceiling**, because the ceiling is Book #1's actual content: real banter pulled from 6 real WAG video transcripts, a real Secret Slime Code mechanic with a real precedent in WAG's own Easter-video countdown device, a real girl-to-lens mapping to be confirmed in the Book Room session, and a real plush character built to a real manufacturability spec.

That is exactly the Humanity Gate test as Katie has framed it: *could another company have generated this exact book from a generic AI prompt? If yes, it fails.* A generic AI prompt could plausibly approximate the craft conventions in this comp shelf — page density, safety-note tone, a spectacle framing device — because those are learnable, genericizable market patterns. It cannot approximate WAG's actual content, because that requires 11 years of a specific, real, three-person relationship, real unpublished memories surfaced in a real recorded Book Room session, and a real illustrator relationship for a real sewn character. The comp shelf's entire job is to make sure Book #1 clears the professional floor these 10 books represent — so that the real, un-generatable content underneath it isn't undersold by amateur execution.
