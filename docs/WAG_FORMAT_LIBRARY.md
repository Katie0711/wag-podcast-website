## FORMAT 1: THE COMMITMENT

**Plain English:** A creator (or the whole cast) publicly locks into one specific rule and states up front exactly how long it lasts or what has to happen for it to end. No script, no plot — the entertainment is 100% "will they actually make it, and what does the rule cost them." The audience isn't told a story, they watch a real bet play out in real time with a stated win/lose condition.

### Real, verified examples

**Ryan Trahan — "I Survived On $0.01 For 30 Days"** — 30M views, 2022-06-01, 19:12
Starts with one literal penny in LA; rule: trade it into as much value as possible over 30 days while crossing the country, tied to a real Feeding America fundraiser. The gap between "started with one cent" and "reach MrBeast in North Carolina" is the whole hook. Escalation: small early trades build momentum, mid-series bigger stunts and near-failures, final days deliver the reveal plus a real donation total. Title formula: *"I Survived On [absurdly small resource] For [stated duration]."* Thumbnail: Trahan and MrBeast in grass, genuine grins, oversized penny prop. **Reusable:** stated tiny start + hard finish line + parallel real-cause scoreboard. **Not reusable:** the Feeding America infrastructure and MrBeast cameo.

**MrBeast — "Last To Leave Circle Wins $500,000"** — 561M views, 2021-08-31, 17:43
100 people in a painted circle; step outside the line, you're out; last person standing wins $500K, up to 14 real days. Escalation is *engineered into the rule itself*: the circle physically shrinks over days, gets quartered by team color, people get cuffed in pairs. Title formula: *"Last To Leave [space] Wins [stake]."* **Reusable:** difficulty that escalates by changing the rule, not just waiting longer; the safe-exit-vs-big-prize tension. **Not reusable:** 100-person cast, $500K prize.

**MrBeast — "7 Days Stranded At Sea"** — 437M views, 2023-08-05
Six people, open ocean, 7 days, two boxes of food, no rescue, voluntarily. Real storm hits day 4, shelter partially destroys. Title formula: *"[Duration] [Trapped state] [Location/condition]."* Thumbnail: "DAY 6" burned directly into the image — the day-counter itself is the packaging device. **Reusable:** day-counter-as-packaging; honest visible cost. **Not reusable:** genuine open-ocean risk, 6-person crew.

### The transferable pattern
Four parts, every time: (1) the rule stated in one sentence, first seconds, zero ambiguity; (2) a hard numeric finish line — a day count, a dollar figure, a destination — never "until we're done"; (3) difficulty engineered to escalate as time passes, the rule itself gets harder; (4) an honest payoff — the video shows the real cost and lets the ending be a genuine struggle, not a clean win. That fourth point is the one already native to WAG's own proven "test honestly, resolve honestly" DNA — this isn't a new muscle, it's the same one stretched over days instead of one segment.

### Title formulas
- *"I [Verb]ED On/For [absurdly limited resource] For [stated duration]"*
- *"[Last/First] To [break the rule] [Wins/Loses] [stated stake]"*
- *"[Stated duration] [Trapped state] [Location/condition]"*

### WAG adaptations
1. **"We Slept At a Different Haunted Texas Location Every Night For 7 Days"** — extends WAG's own proven Overnight Cryptid bucket into a stated commitment with a day-counter.
2. **"We Only Ate 1-Star Texas Restaurants For 7 Days Straight"** — extends WAG's #1 post-pivot performer from one sitting into a full week.
3. **"My Sister Made Every Decision For Me For 7 Days"** — direct duration-extension of WAG's real prior art ("My Sister Decides What We Eat for 24 Hours").
4. **"We Went 7 Days With No Phones"** — teen-relatable, honest stakes, zero production cost, day-counter thumbnails map directly onto the Stranded-At-Sea device.
5. **"We Verified Every Viral TikTok Food Claim For 7 Days"** — only works if the daily honest verify-or-debunk mechanic is the spine, matching WAG's proven claim-test-resolve format — not a bare "try 7 things" container (see Format 3's flop data).

### What could make it fail
The format's payoff depends on the audience believing the rule was never broken or edited around — fragile trust at WAG's production scale. A 3-person, no-crew cast sustaining genuinely unbroken daily footage for a week without visible gaps is a real production risk, not hypothetical: this exact format has drawn "faked challenge" accusations even against Trahan's full crew. Any restrictive-commitment idea involving Annabella (16) needs a built-in safety/wellbeing framing so it reads as a bounded challenge, never as something that could look like neglect on camera.

**Confidence: High.** Three independent real examples, two creators, three different sub-shapes, all directly verified.

## FORMAT 2: VERIFICATION / INVESTIGATION

**Plain English:** A real claim or legend gets stated as a question in the first minute — "is this actually haunted / real / true?" — then the cast physically tests it on camera, and the video ends by actually answering the question, including "we don't know" as a legitimate ending. Same claim-test-resolve shape as WAG's food/restaurant videos, applied to legends instead of menus.

### Real, verified examples

**WAG's own — "24 Hours in America's Most Haunted Hotel! \*Ghost in Room\*"** — 171K views, 33:30
Real chapter structure: 0:00–2:30 legend setup ("This hotel has been called one of the most haunted hotels in Texas") plus a stated "3 Questions We Came to Answer" device; 2:30–5:40 first physical contact with the room; 5:40–10:00 ghost-device testing begins, first ambiguous event; after minute 10, escalating triggered devices, a real turning point ("We said Jesus... and it immediately stopped"), closing on "What REALLY Happened That Night (Final Truth)" — directly answering the opening claim.

**WAG's own — "24 HOURS in the GOATMAN'S FOREST! (It Followed Us!)"** — 11K views, 24:27
Identical chassis, applied to the Lake Worth Monster/Goatman legend. Real description states three yes/no questions, same device as the Hotel video. **The single most important WAG-specific data point in this whole format:** this is WAG's second attempt at the identical chassis, and it did **15x worse** than the first (11K vs. 171K) — a real result, not a hypothetical risk, and not yet diagnosed.

**FaZe Rug — "Our Terrifying Night at World's Most Haunted Museum"** — 9M views, 28:55
Real invitation from a named paranormal investigator (Zach Bagans) with his own museum gives the claim outside credibility before any testing starts. Escalation moves through real objects and testimony, not jump-scare editing.

*Corroborating:* Sam and Colby's entire 16.2M-subscriber channel runs this identical chassis on haunted locations at scale.

### The transferable pattern
Four moves repeat: (1) the legend stated as a real, historical or reported claim, never vague spookiness; (2) a concrete, countable set of questions stated early; (3) a visible testing method carries the middle; (4) the resolution answers the exact question the video opened with, honestly.

### Title formula
*"[Duration] in/at [Superlative] [Place or Legend Name]! ([Payoff tease])"*

### WAG adaptations
1. **"24 Hours Testing the Chupacabra Legend! (Is It Really Out There?)"** — a real, widely-reported South/Central Texas cryptid, genuinely different from the two already-produced entries.
2. **"We Tried to See the Bragg Road Ghost Light! (Real or Not?)"** — a real, documented Texas phenomenon (Saratoga, TX) — an observable light, forcing a stakeout method instead of equipment-in-a-room.
3. **"We Tested the 'Cursed' Viral TikTok Item Everyone Says Is Bad Luck"** — the most portable version: no location access needed, just a stated claim and an honest experiment.

### What could make it fail
Both outside examples that make this work at scale (FaZe Rug, Sam and Colby) lean on a **real outside authority or gatekeeper** for credibility. WAG doesn't have an equivalent yet, and the Goatman result is real evidence that swapping the legend alone doesn't guarantee the format repeats its own success. This format is also physically and logistically heavier than Lane 1 review videos — overnight shoots, two minors present — real overhead that doesn't automatically pay for itself in views.

**Confidence: Medium.** Real multi-source convergence on the mechanism, but WAG-specific effectiveness isn't proven — the second attempt underperformed the first 15x, undiagnosed.

## FORMAT 3: COMPLETION ("EVERY X")

**Plain English:** State a completeness claim right in the title — every fast food place in the city, every country's school lunch — and the whole video is built around whether that claim actually gets delivered. Only works when the category is real and boundable, and there's a built-in clock or physical constraint forcing the completion to actually happen on camera.

### Real, verified examples

**Nick DiGiovanni — "I Ate School Lunch From Every Country"** — 68M views, 2025-10-04, 37:31
A row of trays, each with a national flag, each tasted and tier-ranked. The flag row is a visible checklist of the completion claim. Payoff teased up front: "watch to the end to see my least favorite."

**SIS vs BRO — "We Tried EVERY Fast Food in 24 hours"** — 3.7M views, 2024-06-15, 26:14
Real siblings, hard 24-hour clock, drawn tasks add a scoring layer. The clock — not a tier list — is the escalation engine, and it can actually be missed.

**WAG's own real data (already verified, not re-derived):** winners "I Ate at EVERY WORST Rated Restaurant" (460K) and "I Ate EVERY Viral TikTok Food" (375K), versus the real flop — two "EVERY Viral TikTok Product" videos (~1.7K each). WAG has already run this exact format twice successfully and twice unsuccessfully — the best first-party evidence available anywhere in this library.

### The transferable pattern
The format only works when four things are true together: (1) the category is real, bounded, externally checkable; (2) a real clock or physical constraint does the escalation work; (3) the payoff is teased up front; (4) the thumbnail visually shows the scope as a checklist. WAG's own flops are the clearest disproof: a completion claim with no clock, no verification angle, no visible checklist left the format with nothing to sustain attention. **"Every X" is a container, not a hook by itself.**

### Title formulas
- *"I [verb-past] EVERY [category] [In/At bounded place]!"*
- *"We [verb-past] EVERY [category] in [time constraint]"*
- *"I [verb]ED at EVERY WORST-Rated [category] In [Location]"* — WAG's own strongest variant, stacking a verification claim on top of pure completion.

### WAG adaptations
1. **"We Ate EVERY Food Truck At [a real, recurring Texas food truck park] In One Day!"** — bounded, checkable, no travel.
2. **"We Ranked EVERY 1-Star Restaurant Within [X] Miles of [town]!"** — combines the proven Worst-Rated bucket with a fully Google-Maps-verifiable claim.
3. **"I Ate EVERY Item On the Menu At [an already-verified WORST-rated restaurant from WAG's own catalog]!"** — lowest-risk option, a sequel to a proven winner.

### What could make it fail
WAG's own data already shows the exact failure mode — a bare claim with no clock/checklist/verification wrapper flops. With no travel budget and three people, the "every" category has to stay genuinely small and local or the claim becomes physically undeliverable in one real shoot.

**Confidence: High.** Three fully independent verified examples plus WAG's own positive *and* negative first-party confirmation on this exact format.

## FORMAT 4: COMPETITION / HEAD-TO-HEAD

**Plain English:** Two named, real groups go up against each other on a title-stated binary premise. The video is chopped into short, numbered rounds, each with its own instant winner, and only reveals the overall winner at the end.

### Real, verified examples

All three from **Jordan Matter** (37.5M subs) — the one creator with a real, repeating head-to-head mechanism; Norris Nuts and SIS vs BRO were checked and don't qualify (their formats resolve on arbitrary game outcomes or category-sweep completion, not true head-to-head).

**"Boys vs Girls - Who's The Better Parent?"** — 34M views, 32:17. Stakes stated with zero title-card delay; transcript-confirmed "Round one is the baby crawl round... Round two..." Thumbnail is a literal color-coded scoreboard (pink team labeled, blue team labeled).

**"BEST FRIENDS vs COUPLE: Who's a Better Valentine?"** — 37M views, timed to Valentine's Day. Same chassis, relational premise, calendar-timed release.

**"Ages 1-50 Compete in EXTREME STRENGTH Competition"** — 12M views (still climbing at check). Cold open, verbatim: "Today, my daughter Salish tries to prove that kids are better than adults" — stated in the first 2 seconds.

### The transferable pattern
A binary team-split title that can only be resolved by watching; premise stated immediately; short explicitly numbered rounds each with a fast visible winner; resolves on a comedic, matter-of-fact beat, never forced sentiment.

### Title formulas
- *"[Group A] vs [Group B] - Who's [Better/Best] [At X]?"*
- *"Ages [N]-[N] Compete in [Superlative] [Category] Competition"*

### WAG adaptations — **guardrail applies, read this first**
WAG's real limitation: three cast members, not two pre-built teams. **"Don't let quantified/scoreboard framing drift into engineered competition or rivalry between the sisters without separately vetting that as its own decision — competitive framing among minors who are also siblings carries real risk of reading as manufactured conflict"** (standing WAG guardrail, restated here because this format runs directly into it). Safer configurations:
1. **"Girls vs Dad: Who's The Better Parent (For a Day)?"** — girls vs. a parent, not each other.
2. **"WAG vs. [a real, brand-safe local team] — Who Actually Knows Texas Better?"** — a real outside group, doubles as a possible sponsor tie-in.
3. **"Ages 16-60 Compete in EXTREME [Category] Competition"** — real extended family, no outside recruiting.
4. **"Sisters vs. Best Friends: Who Actually Knows Us Better?"** — puts Angelina and Annabella on the *same* side against real friends — the safer configuration.

**Confidence: Medium-High.** Real, but single-creator — no second independent creator confirms this exact mechanism, and WAG has zero internal prior-art to cross-check.

## FORMAT 5: CONFINEMENT / TRAPPED

**Plain English:** Real people placed inside a clearly bounded, visible space, governed by one simple stated rule that decides success or failure. No script — the space and the rule generate the drama by themselves.

### Real, verified examples

**MrBeast — "I Spent 7 Days In Solitary Confinement"** — 298M views, 2023-12-30, 20:15
Cold open, exact quote: "...to spend the next seven days in solitary confinement." Thumbnail: "DAY 6" burned into the wall — visible-days-passing as the packaging device. Reused verbatim on a 50-hour variant (344M views) — a real two-entry franchise.

**Airrack — "How Many Days Can I Secretly Live In a Grocery Store?"** — 21M views, 2026-01-25, 42 min
Hidden shelf-top shelter in a real, operating store; rules stated before "Day 1"; a real security guard confronts him around 19:52. **Not portable as-is** — requires unsupervised access to someone else's real business, a legal/access risk WAG doesn't have.

**MrBeast — "Last To Leave Circle Wins $500,000"** — 561M views (see Format 1 — appears in both formats because the same mechanism serves both).

### The transferable pattern
One rule, one visible physical edge, no script needed — the boundary itself is the thumbnail's visual subject.

### WAG adaptations
1. **"We Got Locked In Our Garage for 24 Hours (No Phones)"** — real bounded space WAG owns, forced proximity, one stated rule.
2. **"Handcuffed Together Until We Escape the Room"** — bounded, timed variant of WAG's own real prior art ("Handcuffed to My Sister for 24 Hours!").
3. **"How Long Can 3 Girls Survive Locked In a Storm Shelter?"** — a real, regionally normal Texas structure, not a manufactured prop.

### What could make it fail
WAG has three people who already live together and get along — no stranger-friction to mine, so 100% of the tension has to come from the rule itself being genuinely uncomfortable in a real way. Any genuinely bounded/hard-to-exit space needs a visible, obvious safety override for Annabella (16) at all times — WAG doesn't have MrBeast's on-set psychologist or safety crew.

**Confidence: High.** Three independent real examples across two creators, plus WAG's own real prior-art precedent ("Handcuffed to My Sister").

## FORMAT 6: THE REPUTATION VERIFICATION TEST

**Plain English:** Find something with a real, documented bad reputation — a 1-star review, a "worst-rated" label — and put it to an honest, on-camera test. Read the actual complaint out loud, go experience the thing it describes, deliver a real verdict.

### Real, verified examples

**Ryan Trahan — "I Tested 1-Star Restaurants"** — 35.5M views, 2023-08-24, 21:19
Cold open by 0:12: "I have collected the craziest 1 star Yelp reviews across all of America, and I am gonna put them to the test." Rule stated by 0:24: leave a 5-star review at every stop. Reads the actual review text on camera before testing the specific complaint.

**Ryan Trahan — "I Tested 1-Star Hotels (again)"** — 45M views, 2024-04-13
Real chapter markers confirm three-city structure; escalates through a genuinely dangerous property (flagged by a local commenter) to a haunted-hotel bit, with a recurring comic beat (an annoyed security guard) providing relief.

**WAG's own — "I Ate at EVERY WORST Rated Restaurant In a New City!"** — 460K views, 2026-04-12, 42:44
Real, timestamped chapter structure: 0:00–0:45 mission stated; four real restaurants tested against specific real complaints (a texture complaint tested across three bowls of soup, a "bathroom of horrors," a food-safety claim); the fourth stop deliberately inverts the pattern — turns out to be good — landing on a memorable, non-predictable ending rather than confirming the reviews. Thumbnail independently converges on the same "quote the real bad review as the headline" template Trahan uses.

### The transferable pattern
Five parts: a real findable negative review is the premise, stated almost immediately; an explicit rule announced up front; the actual review text is quoted before testing; each stop tests one specific named complaint; the video ends on a verdict, with the strongest examples deliberately breaking the pattern with an honest reversal.

### Title formula
*"I Tested [Negative-Rating Label] [Category]"* (national scale) / *"I [Verb]ED at EVERY WORST-Rated [Category] In [Location]"* (WAG's own, more filmable single-city version).

### WAG adaptations
1. **"We Tested Every 1-Star Rated Nail Salon In Our City!"** — cheap, fast, low liability, natural genuine-reaction reveal.
2. **"We Tried Every 1-Star Rated Thrift Store In Our City!"** — a teen-relevant category neither Trahan nor WAG has used.
3. **"We Booked Texas' Worst-Rated Haunted House and Fact-Checked Every Complaint!"** — merges with the proven cryptid/legend bucket, natural Halloween timing.

### What could make it fail
The real risk is **local blowback, not creative risk.** Trahan operates nationally and never sees a featured business again. WAG's whole review pool is one metro area — the same local businesses their own audience recognizes, and can respond to directly. A version that reads as mean-spirited toward an identifiable small business carries real reputational risk in a way it doesn't for a placeless national creator.

**Confidence: High.** Three independently verified real examples across two channels, same five-part skeleton, corroborated by Trahan running it as a multi-year franchise and WAG independently converging on the identical thumbnail trick.

## FORMAT 7: THE SURPRISE TRANSFORMATION

**Plain English:** Rebuild or redecorate a real space for someone who genuinely doesn't know it's happening, then film their real, unscripted reaction. Before and after are both real; only the information is withheld — same as a surprise party.

### Real, verified examples

**Jordan Matter — "My Daughter's Surprise ROOM MAKEOVER"** — 29M views, 2023-08-12, 18:17
Salish is occupied elsewhere while her brother secretly builds and decorates her actual bedroom. Thumbnail: explicit before/after split-screen, genuine wide-open-mouth reaction.

**Jordan Matter — "Eating & Buying Everything in One Color ft/ epic pink room makeover"** — 48M views (the highest of the three, two months later, same channel)
Same mechanism, stacked with a same-day "only buy/eat pink or black" challenge as a deliberate distraction while the room build happens off-camera. The one real head-to-head comparison available shows the stacked version outperforming the single-thread version — flagged as a real correlation, not a proven cause (sponsor push, thumbnail luck, and seasonal timing are competing explanations that can't be ruled out).

**MrBeast & Beast Philanthropy — "I Built 100 Houses And Gave Them Away!"** — 271M views, 2024-06-29
Structurally different, real before/after at scale — real families, real nonprofit partners, multiple countries. Drew documented public backlash calling the project performative (Newsweek, Unilad).

### The transferable pattern
A real, specific, tangible thing changes state within one video; the "after" is deliberately withheld until one concentrated reveal; titles state the scope explicitly; the reveal is captured with genuine, unrestrained reaction.

### WAG adaptations
1. **"We Surprised Annabella With Her Dream Room Makeover!"** — Angelina and Scarlett secretly redo Annabella's actual bedroom in one real day.
2. **"We Tested Every Viral Drink Recipe While Secretly Building Scarlett's Dream Reading Nook"** — stacks WAG's own proven Viral-Testing engine as the distraction layer, mirroring the one real comparison that favored stacking.

### What could make it fail
Both Jordan Matter proof points lean on a visibly larger budget than WAG's (sponsor-subsidized decor, an outside "special guest" running the build) — an unsponsored WAG version risks a visually smaller "after" that undercuts the thumbnail's own promise, and that gap won't be hideable in editing. Genuine information-asymmetry is also harder with three cast members who share close living space than it was for Matter, who had an outside collaborator run interference. MrBeast's version drew real backlash for reading as performative — never frame a WAG transformation as "helping" someone in a vulnerable situation unless WAG genuinely has the resources and follow-through to back it; keeping the format scoped to the cast's own spaces avoids this entirely.

**Confidence: Medium.** Real, but thinner evidence base — no transcript-level minute-by-minute verification for any of the three examples, and one real recurring sub-series plus one structurally different adjacent example.
