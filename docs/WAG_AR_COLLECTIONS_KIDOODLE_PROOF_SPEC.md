Explicit standard: "I think we've emailed them three times" is not an acceptable answer once this exists — the system should know exactly.
2. **Determine the current collection stage** against a pressure-tested escalation ladder (starting point given: INVOICED → DUE → OVERDUE → FRIENDLY FOLLOW-UP → SECOND FOLLOW-UP → INTERNAL ESCALATION → FORMAL PAYMENT REQUEST → EXECUTIVE ESCALATION → CONTRACTUAL REMEDY REVIEW → RESOLVED, improvable if evidence supports something better) — and say exactly where Kidoodle sits and why. A company 5 days late is not the same case as one that's ignored 5 documented contacts for 60 days.
3. **Separate relationship value from collection risk explicitly** — score outstanding amount, days overdue, unanswered-contact count, contractual clarity, payment history, relationship value, renewal/future-deal value, whether a genuine dispute exists, administrative-vs-concerning read, and concentration/exposure risk, separately — then recommend the *lowest* escalation level likely to actually produce payment without unnecessarily damaging a real partner relationship. Protecting the relationship is not license to let invoices sit unpaid indefinitely, and neither failure mode should win by default.
4. **Design the permanent post-deal revenue loop**: `OPPORTUNITY → VERIFY → APPROVE → CONTACT → NEGOTIATE → CONTRACT → DELIVER → INVOICE → PAYMENT DUE → PAYMENT RECEIVED → RECONCILE → RELATIONSHIP/RENEWAL → LEARN`. Minimum question set the eventual structure must answer on demand: who owes WAG money, how much, for what, when was it due, how late, who owns collection, what was the last contact, what did they say, what's the next action, when, does Katie need to decide anything, what's the relationship risk.

6. **AR scoreboard concept** — eventual "what money are we waiting on" answer: total outstanding, aging buckets (current, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+), invoices needing follow-up, non-responding companies, promised payment dates, broken promises, disputes, collected-this-month, average days-to-payment, undelivered-but-unbilled work.
