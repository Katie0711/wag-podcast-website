## Why this category matters (the reframe, stated plainly)

Every card in `WAG_SPONSOR_OPPORTUNITY_CARDS.md` is a **single brand relationship** — each one took real research to find, and each one is a single door. The entities in this document are structurally different: they are **routing layers** that sit between advertiser budgets and dozens or hundreds of shows/creators at once. One real relationship with a podcast ad-sales network or a media-buying agency doesn't just open one brand — it opens WAG's inventory (podcast ad slots, YouTube integrations) up to whichever of their existing advertiser clients happens to be looking for teen/Gen-Z-female reach that quarter. That's the scale argument. The honest tradeoff, disclosed up front: **every one of these intermediaries also gates on scale, curation, or an existing relationship more aggressively than a single brand does** — a brand can fall in love with WAG's specific content; a network or agency is optimizing a portfolio and has less reason to make an exception for a smaller show. That tension is why the priority calls below matter more here than in the brand-card document.

# SECTION 1 — Podcast Ad-Sales Networks / Reps

## 1. Spotify Audience Network (SAN) — via Spotify for Podcasters (formerly Anchor)

- **What they actually do:** A programmatic audio-ad marketplace that connects advertisers to listeners across Spotify Originals, Megaphone-hosted enterprise podcasts, *and* podcasts hosted via Spotify for Podcasters (the free, open-to-anyone hosting tool, formerly Anchor). Advertisers buy against demographic/geographic/behavioral targeting and contextual show-category targeting — not against a specific show's raw download count, which is the mechanic that makes it viable for smaller shows.
- **Audience/scale typically required:** No confirmed minimum download or audience-size threshold was found in this research. The real, stated design intent — explicitly, in Spotify's own materials — is to let smaller/emerging podcasts that are "routinely passed over by advertisers" capture the same demand pool as larger shows, because advertisers are buying against audience data, not raw scale.
- **Advertiser categories represented:** Not category-restricted — self-serve buying via Spotify Ad Studio spans any advertiser willing to buy programmatically; real ad-tech infrastructure supporting "advertisers of all sizes."
- **How emerging shows get in:** **Self-serve, no referral required.** A show hosted on Spotify for Podcasters can request/enroll in SAN monetization directly through the platform's own dashboard — a documented technical process (ensuring at least one ad slot, pre/mid-roll), not a pitch or an application to a gatekeeper.
- **Revenue split:** 50/50, confirmed.
- **Real caveat, disclosed:** This is programmatic remnant-style inventory, not curated brand-safety-vetted sponsorships — WAG would not be choosing which specific advertisers run, and ad quality/relevance varies more than a hand-sold placement. It is also genuinely small-dollar at WAG's likely early volume; this is a low-effort revenue stream, not a strategic relationship.
- **WAG priority assessment: HIGH — worth doing now, not "watch for later."** This is the one intermediary in this entire document WAG could plausibly turn on without any outreach, pitch, or relationship-building at all, simply by confirming `WAG Podcast` is hosted (or migrated to be hosted) via Spotify for Podcasters and opting into SAN. It doesn't compete with or block pursuing any of the higher-upside relationships below — it's closer to "turn on a revenue tap" than "business development."

## 2. Acast Marketplace + Acast Creative Studios

- **What they actually do:** Acast Marketplace is a self-serve programmatic/host-read ad marketplace matching podcasts with advertisers; Acast Creative Studios is Acast's in-house branded-content production arm — real enough that it built and co-launched BetterHelp's own first original podcast ("Mind If We Talk?") in a full production partnership, not just an ad placement.
- **Audience/scale typically required:** Real, specific, confirmed threshold: **1,000+ monthly listeners** to apply to the Marketplace; most creators reportedly begin seeing real revenue around 5,000 monthly listeners.
- **Advertiser categories represented:** Broad — DTC and enterprise; BetterHelp (mental health, one of the exact advertiser categories WAG's own research already flagged as buying against this demographic) is a confirmed, real, current Acast partner at the production-studio level, not just an ad buyer.
- **How emerging shows get in:** **Self-serve application** through the Acast dashboard's Marketplace tab ("Apply to the marketplace"), plus a separate lighter-weight "Creator Profiles" monetization option.
- **Real caveat, disclosed:** The Marketplace/Creator Profile self-serve tier is real but modest-scale; access to something like Acast Creative Studios' full branded-podcast production relationships (the BetterHelp-level deals) is not confirmed to be self-serve — that tier likely requires a direct sales conversation once a show has real scale.
- **WAG priority assessment: MEDIUM-HIGH — worth applying to the self-serve Marketplace now** (WAG Podcast likely already clears 1,000 monthly listeners), while treating Acast Creative Studios' deeper studio relationships as a later-stage watch item once WAG Podcast has more scale and a track record.

## 3. Podcorn

- **What they actually do:** A creator marketplace using algorithmic matching (budget, creative format, target podcaster profile) to connect podcasters directly with brands for native/host-read advertising and branded content.
- **Audience/scale typically required:** No confirmed minimum found; positioned explicitly as accessible to podcasters of varying size, not enterprise-gated.
- **Advertiser categories represented:** Broad DTC/consumer brand marketplace; specific teen/Gen-Z-female advertiser presence not independently confirmed in this pass.
- **How emerging shows get in:** Self-serve sign-up; brands and podcasters both create profiles and the platform's matching handles introductions. Podcorn takes a **10% cut** of ad revenue (lower than Gumball's, see below).
- **Real caveat, disclosed:** As a broad self-serve marketplace, deal flow and advertiser quality/fit are less predictable than a curated network — WAG would be one of many thousands of listed shows, not individually pitched to advertisers.
- **WAG priority assessment: MEDIUM — a legitimate low-effort revenue channel worth setting up alongside SAN/Acast, but not a strategic relationship in the way a curated network or agency contact would be.**
