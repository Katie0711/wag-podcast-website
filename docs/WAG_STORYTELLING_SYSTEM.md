# WAG Storytelling System

**Part of the WAG hit-making system project (started 2026-08-08), which runs after the Channel Publishing QA rollout per Katie's explicit sequencing.** This document owns the *narrative* half of that project — the invisible structure underneath WAG's unscripted content, and a library of repeatable, named episode formats built from it. The title/thumbnail half lives in `WAG_PACKAGING_INTELLIGENCE.md` (see the two new pattern entries added there this pass). The idea-generation half lives in `WAG_CONTENT_BUCKETS.md`. The pre-production filter that reads all three lives in `WAG_VIDEO_GREENLIGHT.md`.

**Hard constraint carried through every section below: no scripted dialogue.** The girls don't want it and the team agrees — every mechanic here has to generate real stakes and real structure from a rule-set, a real relationship, or a real constraint, never from written lines. Where a studied creator's format leans on production budget or scale WAG doesn't have, that's flagged, not silently copied.

**Success framing:** this system is meant to systematically raise the odds of a strong episode and catch weak ideas before filming — not guarantee a hit. See `WAG_VIDEO_GREENLIGHT.md` for the explicit no-fake-certainty-score guardrail.

## The invisible structure

Every creator studied — despite wildly different budgets and genres — builds episodes on the same seven-beat skeleton. None of it requires a script; all of it requires *planning the container*, not the dialogue.

1. **Premise + real stakes, stated fast (0–15s).** Ryan Trahan states the rule-set and a "Game Plan" almost immediately. Airrack's titles do the premise-stating before the video even starts. The lesson for WAG: don't build up to the premise — say what's actually happening and what's actually at stake in the first few seconds, then let the video deliver on it.
2. **A fixed container, not a plot.** The format is a rule-set, a location, a time limit, or a prize — something real people can improvise inside. (Penny Challenge's trade-up rule; "how many days in X"; "24 hours doing Y.") WAG already does this instinctively in its overnight-cryptid videos (Bigfoot, Goatman, haunted hotel) — the container is real, the outcome isn't predetermined.
3. **Stepwise escalation with real mini-goals.** Not a flat middle — each checkpoint raises stakes or narrows the odds (Ryan Trahan's compounding trade-ups; Airrack's advancing day-count before getting caught).
4. **Real relationship stakes, not invented characters.** This is Salish Matter's mechanic and it is WAG's single biggest natural advantage over every creator studied here — three real sisters with a real, ongoing relationship already existing on camera. A dare or a debate format doesn't need manufactured drama when the people already have real history.
5. **A manufactured reversal or consequence device — never a fabricated one.** Ryan Trahan's "Great Reset" and FaZe Rug's real bans/losses show that tension can be built into the rules of the format itself (a real risk of failure, a real prize on the line, a real time limit) rather than staged. WAG's content-authenticity rule means this has to be a real risk the format creates, not a plot twist inserted after the fact.
6. **An emotional beat at the end, not a moral.** Salish Matter's "Who Knows Me Better?" closes on a real reaction — vindication or a "you really do know me" surprise — matching WAG's own already-established Stories calibration (memorable ending, not a lesson box). Carry this same standard into video pacing, not just written articles.
7. **A retention-aware pace, honestly earned.** Front-load curiosity, withhold the resolution, but the payoff has to match the setup — every source flagged this same risk (over-promising in the title/hook and under-delivering in the payoff reads as clickbait, which is the one thing WAG's sponsor-facing credibility can't absorb).

**What doesn't transfer, and why:** Ryan Trahan's daily-episode/12-hour-edit-day cadence and cross-country production scale assume a production budget and crew WAG doesn't have — the *mechanic* (rule-set + compounding stakes) transfers, the *cadence* doesn't. FaZe Rug's haunted-location mini-series depends on a fixed, recurring real location (his "Haunted Tunnel") built up over years — worth naming as a long-term asset-building idea, not something to replicate in one video.

## Episode Framework Library

Named, repeatable formats — each one a container, not a one-off idea, built to hold many future episodes the same way the studied creators' formats do. Existing WAG franchises are listed first since the discipline here is *strengthen what's already proven* before inventing new ones (per the WAG five-pillars tiebreaker). New formats are proposed only where a real, evidence-backed gap exists.

### Already-proven WAG formats (deepen these first)

| Format | Real stakes source | What this research adds |
|---|---|---|
| **WAG Verdict** (dating/red-flags debate) | Real opinions on real scenarios | Salish Matter's reveal-beat structure — end each verdict on a real reaction shot, not just a stated conclusion |
| **WAG Predicted It** | Real prediction vs. real outcome | Ryan Trahan's stepwise-escalation device — show the prediction being tracked/updated at real checkpoints before the reveal, not just before/after |
| **WAG Game Night** (party-game umbrella) | Real in-the-moment reactions | Salish Matter's "Who Knows Me Better?" mechanic is a direct, ready-to-run addition to this umbrella — real sister-vs-friend or sister-vs-sister pairings, swappable every episode |
| **Guys Answer Questions** | A real guest's real answers | Airrack's container logic — treat "which guy, which questions" as swappable variables inside one fixed format, exactly like "how many days in ___" |
| **Overnight cryptid challenges** (Bigfoot, Goatman, haunted hotel) | Real overnight risk/discomfort | FaZe Rug's recurring-location idea — if WAG ever returns to the same real location a second time, treat it as a deliberate franchise beat ("we went back to..."), not a coincidence |

### New formats worth piloting (real gap, real production feasibility, Texas-based, no scripting)

1. **"Who Knows [Sister] Better?"** — direct adaptation of Salish Matter's format. Two real people (a sister vs. a real friend, or sister vs. sister) answer questions about a third real sister; stakes come entirely from the real relationships already in place. Zero new production requirement — same setup as WAG Verdict.
2. **"How Many [X] Can We ___ In One Day?"** — Airrack/FaZe Rug's "EVERY" completeness-claim container, adapted to WAG's existing viral-TikTok-testing format (already a proven WAG bucket) rather than invented fresh. Swappable variable each episode (every viral snack, every 1-star place in a new city — WAG already has "I Ate at EVERY WORST Rated Restaurant" and the TikTok-testing episodes as real prior art).
3. **A real, escalating stakes-tracker for overnight challenges** — borrow Ryan Trahan's checkpoint structure (not his cadence) to give the cryptid/overnight format a clearer mid-video structure: state the plan up front, hit 2-3 real checkpoints on camera, close on a real reaction, not just a flat "nothing happened" ending. This is a pacing fix to an existing format, not a new one.

**Explicitly not recommended:** a scripted mystery/investigation narrative in the FaZe Rug style — his haunted-location mini-series depends on a built-up recurring real location and years of continuity WAG hasn't built yet; forcing a serialized mystery plot now would risk drifting toward invented drama, which breaks the content-authenticity rule this project holds to.

## Real use, round 1 (2026-08-08)

The Episode Framework Library stopped being theoretical the same day it was written: 10 real candidate ideas were scored through `WAG_VIDEO_GREENLIGHT.md` (full log there). Real findings from that first round:

- **"Who Knows [Sister] Better?" — validated as worth a real pilot, not just a good idea on paper.** Its first scored candidate ("Who Knows Annabella Better: Her Sisters or Her Best Friend?") scored 12/14, in the same range as WAG's already-proven franchises — real, first evidence the Salish Matter mechanic transfers cleanly, pending an actual filmed outcome.
- **Every green-lit idea this round used a real, statable container** (a rule-set, a real legend + location, a real relationship pairing) — the one idea with no real container (#10, a pure travel premise with nothing generating real stakes) scored dramatically lower (4/14) than everything else. First real confirmation that "container, not plot" isn't just a nice framing — it's the actual thing separating a fundable idea from a weak one.
- **Not yet learned:** none of Round 1's ideas have been filmed yet, so nothing here is validated by real outcome data — only by internal consistency with WAG's own prior real-performance patterns. Update this section again once Round 1 (or a subset of it) actually publishes.
