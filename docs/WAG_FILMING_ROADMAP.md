# WAG Filming & Release Roadmap

**One executive question this module answers: in what order should these actually get filmed?**

**Part of the WAG YouTube Growth Operating System.** This does not repeat what's already written — full per-idea packaging (title reasoning, thumbnail concept, hook, story framework, escalation, ending, comparable creator evidence, production notes, risks) lives in `WAG_CREATOR_PLAYBOOK.md`, Section 12. This document adds the fields that section doesn't cover — purpose, hypothesis, ceiling, confidence, cost/effort, and a real 1–10 filming order — and turns the Round 2 Greenlight log (`WAG_VIDEO_GREENLIGHT.md`) into an actual sequence, not just a scored list.

**The ranking logic, stated plainly:** this is not sorted by score alone. A ranked-by-score-only list would front-load every proven idea and back-load every experiment, which isn't how a real slate should be built — it needs proven wins on the calendar early (cash flow of attention, so to speak) *and* the one real experimental swing placed deliberately, not buried last or rushed first. The order below balances real confidence, real production logistics (what needs advance booking), and deliberate variety so three overnight-legend videos or three worst-rated videos don't all cluster together.

**Why #5 (Trapped) sits mid-slate, not first or last:** it's this round's one real experimental bet — the only idea built from a pattern none of WAG's existing franchises currently use. Filming it 5th means two proven wins (Guys Answer Questions, Who Knows Annabella Better) and two franchise extensions are already on the board first, so the channel isn't leaning on an unproven format early, but it's not held so late that the whole slate plays it safe. If it works, it's real evidence for a new franchise; if it underperforms, the postmortem is cheap because four other real wins are already banked around it.

**Why the two overnight-legend videos (#3, #7) aren't adjacent:** both need real advance location-access booking — spacing them lets prep happen in parallel with filming the videos ranked between them, rather than compressing two location-logistics dependencies back to back.

**Why the two Worst-Rated videos (#9, #10) both land near the bottom:** not because they're weak — they're real, reliable bucket-fillers — but per the round's own honest note, #10 specifically has "lower ceiling than the others... a reliable, low-cost bucket-filler." Real, proven ideas with a real, honestly-lower ceiling belong at the end of a slate, not because they're bad ideas, but because a roadmap that's honest about ceiling puts its highest-upside real bets earlier.

## Per-idea roadmap fields (new fields only — see `WAG_CREATOR_PLAYBOOK.md` Section 12 for full packaging)

### 1. Guys Answer Questions: Red Flags
- **Purpose:** bank a proven, reliable win and keep the franchise's real cadence alive.
- **Hypothesis:** repeats the channel's real highest-recommendation-share format; should perform in line with the franchise's own real prior episodes.
- **Budget band:** low — one real local guest, no location cost.
- **Prep burden:** low — mainly booking a real, willing, articulate guest.
- **Location/cast requirements:** one real local guest; no special location.
- **Filming/editing difficulty:** low filming (conversation-format), low-moderate editing (real-time follow-up pacing).
- **Mid-video re-engagement:** a real, unplanned follow-up question the moment the guest says something unexpected — the format's own real strength already does this naturally.
- **Shorts opportunity:** high — single sharp real answers cut cleanly as standalone Shorts, already the franchise's proven pattern.
- **Website/article opportunity:** direct — feeds the existing, real `guys-answer-questions` hub on thewagpodcast.com.
- **Sponsor fit:** strong — conversational format absorbs a real sponsor mention without breaking pacing.
- **What success teaches:** confirms the franchise's real durability with a fresh guest/topic. **What failure teaches:** would be the first real signal the format needs a new angle, not just a new guest.

### 2. Who Knows Annabella Better: Sisters vs. Best Friend
- **Purpose:** pilot WAG's single biggest structural advantage (real sisters, real relationship) in a validated outside mechanic.
- **Hypothesis:** should land in the same real performance range as WAG's other 12-13/14 ideas, with a real chance to outperform given the strength of the underlying real-relationship pattern.
- **Budget band:** low — no location cost, needs the real best friend's real availability.
- **Prep burden:** moderate — real trivia-round questions need real advance thought (not scripted answers, but a real structure), and a non-parent should run the questioning per the Jordan Matter production lesson.
- **Location/cast requirements:** one real friend as a guest; home/studio location.
- **Filming/editing difficulty:** low-moderate filming, moderate editing (needs real pacing across escalating rounds).
- **Mid-video re-engagement:** the round where questions get "more personal/specific" (already planned) IS the mid-video beat.
- **Shorts opportunity:** high — individual trivia rounds and the final reveal cut cleanly.
- **Website/article opportunity:** real — could seed a new "Who Knows [Name] Better" hub if the pilot performs, but don't build the hub before the pilot has real data.
- **Sponsor fit:** moderate — a trivia-format sponsor integration (e.g., a real prize sponsor) fits naturally if one exists.
- **What success teaches:** validates a real new franchise built on WAG's own structural advantage — worth pursuing "Who Knows [Name] Better" as a repeatable format. **What failure teaches:** the mechanic may need a bigger real stakes/prize element to land the way it does for Salish Matter.

### 5. Trapped: 24 Hours, One Room, Three Sisters
