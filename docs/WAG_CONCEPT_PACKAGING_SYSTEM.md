## The canonical concept workflow

```
WORLD-CLASS OUTLIER / WAG LEARNING / CURRENT PLATFORM SIGNAL
  → UNDERLYING PSYCHOLOGY
  → WAG-NATIVE PREMISE
  → PREMISE STRENGTH
  → CURIOSITY GAP
  → TITLE
  → THUMBNAIL
  → PROMISE
  → HOOK
  → ESCALATION / FUTURE PAYOFF
  → STORY ARCHITECTURE
  → GREENLIGHT
  → FILM → EDIT → PUBLISH → MEASURE → LEARN
```

This runs *before* filming, feeding the existing Greenlight gate (`WAG_GREENLIGHT_MANAGER_SPEC.md`) — it does not replace it.

## Surface formula ≠ strategy — the mandatory distinction

"SECRET," "MISSING," "EVERY," "STOLEN," "WORST," etc. are surface expressions of deeper mechanisms, not strategies themselves, and may only be used when truthful to what was actually filmed. Every packaging recommendation must trace: **surface formula → psychological mechanism → underlying premise architecture → WAG-native translation.** Mechanically copying trigger words without this chain is explicitly prohibited.

## 1. Premise Bucket — classify the story engine before writing a title

| Bucket | Story engine |
|---|---|
| Mystery | Missing, stolen, imposter, who did it |
| Secret | Hiding, sneaking, secret rooms/identities, secretly doing/living/trying something |
| Escalation | Every round gets harder/scarier/bigger/older/worse |
| Transformation | Person/place/object changes dramatically |
| Survival | Survive, last to leave, endure, escape |
| Rescue/Discovery | Lost animals/people, finding, saving, adopting |
| Relationship Drama | Sisters, friends, crushes, dating, parents, breakups |
| Competition | X vs Y, teams, ages, strongest, last to quit |
| Trend Brought to Life | Existing cultural/game/movie trend turned into a real-world story |
| Impossible Build | Secret rooms, extreme builds, impossible locations |
| Investigation/Exposure | Testing, exposing, proving, debunking |

**Expandable, not fixed** — the taxonomy must be able to discover new premise structures from future outliers, never hard-coded as final.

## 2. Curiosity Gap — every package withholds exactly one piece of information

| Gap type | Viewer question |
|---|---|
| Identity Gap | Who did it? Which person? |
| Outcome Gap | Who wins? Does she survive? |
| Discovery Gap | What did they find? What's inside? |
| Explanation Gap | Why did this happen? What went wrong? |
| Reaction Gap | How will she react? |
| Progression Gap | How far does this escalate? |

For every title candidate: **what ONE question enters the viewer's brain?** No obvious question = flag the package as weak.

## 3. The Every-Bucket Test

For a promising concept, do not generate five minor variations of the same title. Force the concept through every relevant bucket, then score the candidates. The point isn't forcing an inappropriate formula — it's discovering whether a different story lens makes the *same footage* dramatically more compelling. Any candidate requiring events that didn't actually happen gets marked **NOT SUPPORTED BY FOOTAGE/PREMISE** — never recommend fabricated drama because a title scores well. This is the operating distinction between two modes:
- **Existing-Footage Mode:** cannot invent events — find the strongest truthful story already in the footage, label unsupported buckets.
- **Pre-Production Mode:** can redesign the shoot itself if a stronger psychological engine exists.

## 4. Score premise strength separately from title strength — never averaged into one hidden number

Premise strength · Curiosity · Clarity · Emotional stakes · Escalation · Thumbnail potential · WAG fit · Trend relevance · Freshness · Character/personality potential · Retention architecture · **Future-Payoff Strength** (at any early point, what valuable/escalated outcome does the viewer anticipate by continuing to watch?). A mediocre premise cannot become a 10/10 by inserting SECRET/MISSING/EVIL — score dimensions individually, display them individually.

## 5. The Promise Chain

**Title creates question → Thumbnail adds new information/tension → Opening confirms promise → Story advances promise → Payoff resolves promise.**

Title and thumbnail have different jobs — never evaluate them in isolation, and never let the thumbnail simply repeat the title. The video must keep the title's question alive and ultimately satisfy it; CTR and retention are connected through this chain, not independent metrics.

## 6. Novelty/Derivation check — clone psychology, not videos

Compare proposed WAG concepts against source inspiration. Flag copied nouns, setting, sequence, gimmick, thumbnail composition, or title wording. Acceptable reuse: escalation, mystery, competition, identity gap, progression, survival, secrecy, relationship stakes — the *mechanism*, not the specific execution.

## 7. Anti-pattern detection — flag before filming, not after

Over-description · no curiosity · generic YouTube language · empty hype · too many concepts stuffed into one title · thumbnail/title duplication · fake stakes (SECRET/MISSING/STOLEN when the footage doesn't support it) · weak premise disguised by strong wording · a title that creates a question the footage can't satisfy.

## 8. Competitor/Outlier Intelligence — Outlier Multiple, not raw views

**Outlier Multiple = video performance ÷ creator's own normal performance.** A 2M-view video on a channel that normally gets 500K is a stronger signal than 10M on a channel that normally gets 15M. This is not a new capability — Creator & Platform Intelligence's real Proof 0 already reasons this way (its real, evidenced finding that "rank movement ≠ absolute performance" caught a real data-integrity bug this session) — this document adopts "Outlier Multiple" as the formal name for that existing discipline, not a new build.

Study successes *and* failures. Ask: does the mechanism transfer across creators, does it transfer to WAG, does it still work currently, under what circumstances does it fail? Graduate from "top creators do this" to "this mechanism repeatedly appears among world-class outliers, WAG has tested it X times, here are our results, here is our current WAG-specific conclusion" — that accumulated conclusion is proprietary WAG Media intelligence, per the existing Experiment Registry (`WAG_EXPERIMENT_REGISTRY.md`) and prediction-vs-actual discipline.

## 9. WAG Translation Layer — not imitation

WAG is not trying to become Rebecca Zamolo, Ben Azelart, or MrBeast. Extract the psychology, translate to WAG's own worlds: Adventure/Investigation (Bigfoot, legends, overnight investigations), Teen Life (school, crushes, friendships), Experiential Challenges (Disney, theme parks, travel), Personality/Comedy (Angelina/Bella/Scarlett dynamics), Podcast (opinions, reactions, relationships). For every external pattern: what's the underlying psychology, and how does WAG express that natively, not as a copy?

## 10. Real reference title patterns — YouTube Title Pattern Playbook (external creator sample, cited not copied)

Ten real title buckets with real proof-point videos (Something Is Wrong, Secret/Hidden/Sneaking, Escalation/Every Time, I Built the Impossible, Exposing/Completing Everything, Relationships+Emotional Stakes, Found/Rescue/Lost, Versus/Imposter, Survival/Challenge, Trend+Story Conflict) — full detail retained in the source playbook, not reproduced here to avoid duplicate-truth risk. Core principle carried forward: **the title is not a label for the video, it's the opening line of the story** — sell what happened *because* you did the activity, not the activity itself.

## 11. The system must push back

Not an agreement machine. If a concept is weak, say so. If the title is stronger than the actual video, say so. If a different story hiding in the footage is stronger than the stated premise, surface it. If WAG is copying a competitor too closely, flag it. If the payoff is weak, identify it *before* filming, not after.
