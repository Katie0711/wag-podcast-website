## 1. Precise concept

Three real restaurants — the worst-rated by current, real Google rating within a stated radius of WAG's home base, each with enough real review volume to rule out a single troll review — get visited in one production day, worst-rating last. At each stop, the girls read one specific real 1-star complaint out loud on camera *before* ordering, then order and eat the exact dish or experience item that complaint describes (not a random menu item), and deliver an honest, specific verdict on that exact complaint — not a blanket judgment of the whole restaurant. Visits happen in real ascending severity order, decided from real ratings data *before* filming. The girls leave a real, honest review at each stop (not a guaranteed 5-star one) as the on-camera, checkable action that closes each segment. The ending is allowed to genuinely surprise: if the "worst" stop isn't the worst experience, that's the real ending, not a scripted one.

This is explicitly **not** a re-description of the original video, and explicitly **not** the naive sequel already sitting in WAG's own idea log (`video_ideas.id=1`, "I Ate EVERY Item on the Menu at [WAG's Own Worst-Rated Restaurant]" — same location, same menu-completion chassis, greenlit on view-count logic alone). Source: `WAG_GREENLIGHT_RESTAURANT_REPUTATION_TEST.md` Section 0, which flags that naive-sequel record as "the exact anti-pattern this task exists to override" and recommends marking it superseded rather than filming it. This card assumes that recommendation stands.

## 8. Structure (beat-by-beat, per WAG's Storytelling System)

Mapped to `WAG_STORYTELLING_SYSTEM.md`'s seven-beat invisible structure and the Opening/Mission/Conflict/Escalation/Twist/Lowest Point/Recovery/Ending framework:

1. **Premise + stakes stated fast (0:00-0:30).** See Section 7 above.
2. **Fixed container, not a plot.** The rule-set: three real restaurants, real lowest-rating tier within a stated radius, one specific complaint tested per stop, a real review left at each stop. Real people improvising inside a real, bounded container — not a script.
3. **Stop 1 (least-controversial 1-star).** Review quoted on camera in full → order/experience the specific complained-about item → deliver an honest, specific verdict on that complaint only → leave a real review on camera.
4. **Stop 2 (mid-severity).** Same structure, repeated as the re-engagement device — each stop is its own mini claim-test-resolve unit, per WAG's own proven Lane 1 chassis (`WAG_CREATIVE_FORMULA_V1.md` Question 3).
5. **Midpoint beat.** Per `WAG_STORYTELLING_SYSTEM.md`'s Advanced Structure section, a real, unscripted moment of doubt or disagreement between the girls about whether the ratings are holding up — not manufactured, whatever actually happens on the day.
6. **Stop 3 (most-complained-about, saved for last).** Highest tension point; the escalation is a real pre-production decision built from real, checkable review data, not a retrofit invented after filming.
7. **Ending/payoff.** All three honest, specific verdicts delivered back to back, closing on whichever stop most surprised the girls and why — with an explicit, non-forced acknowledgment if the "worst" rated stop wasn't actually the worst experience. No manufactured bow.
8. **Emotional beat, not a moral.** Matches `WAG_STORYTELLING_SYSTEM.md` beat 6 and WAG's own Stories calibration standard (memorable ending, not a lesson box).

Source: `WAG_GREENLIGHT_RESTAURANT_REPUTATION_TEST.md` Section 3 ("Full concept," Rules 1-8), `WAG_STORYTELLING_SYSTEM.md`.

## 12. Risks (real, specific)

1. **Local blowback.** WAG's review pool is one metro area its own audience recognizes and can respond to directly — a stop that reads as mean-spirited toward an identifiable small business carries real reputational risk. Mitigation already built into the concept: test the specific complaint, not the whole business (Section 1, Rule 4).
2. **Ratings don't hold up by shoot day.** A 1-star restaurant can change ownership, fix the exact complaint, or simply not be as bad as an old review claims. Ratings must be re-verified close to the shoot date.
3. **Insufficient real review volume in the metro area.** If the area doesn't have three real, separately-earned 1-star ratings, the "worst-rated" claim breaks and shouldn't be forced.
4. **Escalation order breaks against real filming.** If the pre-planned severity order doesn't match what's actually experienced, the video must report what happened, not the plan — this protects truthfulness but complicates the pre-cut structure and the thumbnail's promise if not planned for in advance.
5. **Food-safety/liability risk.** Genuine, distinct from and more real than any "survival" title framing (deliberately rejected in Section 4 above) — needs real safety planning independent of the on-camera premise.
6. **Repeat-formula fatigue at the mechanism level.** This is WAG's third real trip through the worst-rated/verification well. The concept and container are genuinely different from both prior entries, but running this exact chassis again soon risks the format itself reading as a rut, separate from any individual title's freshness.
7. **Channel-baseline mismatch with expectations.** The channel's real current long-form baseline (median 12K views) is far below the original's 460.9K peak. If the girls or Katie go in expecting a repeat of the historical number, a real, solid result well below that peak could get misread as underperformance rather than a successful format test.
8. **Two of three cast members are minors.** Any thumbnail or on-camera reaction must be genuine, not staged for effect — `WAG_CREATIVE_FORMULA_V1.md` Question 9's explicit guardrail applies to this shoot the same as any other.
