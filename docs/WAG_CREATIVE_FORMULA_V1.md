# WAG Creative Formula v1

**Scope: WAG Main (wildadventuregirls.com / @TheWildAdventureGirls), not WAG Podcast** — the two remain separate companies per standing rule; this formula does not govern podcast content.

**What this document is not:** another report. It's the working creative philosophy for WAG Main going forward — the thing Greenlight scores against, the thing the next 25 videos come from, the thing a future agent or a future hire can read cold and know what a WAG video is supposed to be. Per Katie's explicit instruction, this does not wait on YouTube Studio access — it's built from WAG's own three transcript-verified videos, five elite creators' real evidence (transcripts, interviews, talks, title/thumbnail history), and independent teen-audience research (Pew, UCLA Teens & Screens, Common Sense Media, a live comment pull). When Studio data becomes available, it refines this — it doesn't gate it.

**How this was built:** the prior pass treated one mechanism (the claim -> test -> resolve "Verification Engine," found in all 3 of WAG's own analyzed videos) as the center of the project. That was a mistake in altitude, not in the underlying research — the Verification Engine turns out to be WAG's own real execution of something bigger: a *reusable chassis, swappable subject matter* pattern that shows up independently in all 5 elite creators studied (MrBeast, Ryan Trahan, Airrack, FaZe Rug, Jordan Matter/Salish Matter), the single clearest "mechanism, not genre" evidence in the whole dataset. This document works one level up from any single mechanism: what repeats across all of them, what's specific to one of them, and what that means WAG should actually become.

## 1. Who is our intended audience?

**The intended audience** (a real, stated choice, not yet measured against WAG's own subscribers): US teens, core 13-17, addressable up through the college-age overlap the platform itself blends in — heavy, cross-platform YouTube users (90% of US teens use it, 73% daily) who are simultaneously growing more ambivalent about the platforms they're on (45% now say they spend too much time on social media, up from 27% in 2023). This is the audience WAG's post-horse-content pivot already committed to; this research grounds it in real external data rather than leaving it as an assumption.

**What's evidenced vs. what's a working hypothesis, stated plainly:** every number above is general US teen research, not WAG's own analytics. **Correction, 2026-08-10 — this isn't a total data void.** `WAG_MEASUREMENT_STANDARDS.md` has real, live-pulled Studio numbers (views, watch time, subscriber trend, average percentage viewed, traffic-source breakdown, 2026-08-09) — WAG is not starting from zero. What's genuinely missing is narrower than "audience data": age/gender demographic breakdown, a real minute-by-minute retention curve (vs. the one summary "average % viewed" stat Studio's simplified view shows), and comment-level analysis of WAG's own channel specifically (the 6-video/120-comment pull cited throughout this document is from *other* creators' channels, not WAG's). Treat those three specific gaps as the target for Studio's deeper report-building flow — not a reason to wait before building.

## 2. What emotional experience are we trying to create?

**WAG makes you feel like you're watching people who'd actually have your back, put through something real.** Three converging, independently-sourced findings build this:

- Teens reward **relatable realism over fantasy** (up 35.3% year-over-year) and **friendship over romance** (59.7%) — UCLA 2025.
- The single highest-liked comment WAG-adjacent research found across a live 6-video pull (41K likes) was a joke born from *real, unscripted tension* — hosts caught being braver about ghosts than bats — not a scripted bit. Comedy that comes from genuine reaction beats comedy performed for the camera.
- The audience consistently rewards **fairness and recognition** over plot: who was kind, who was left out, who quietly carried the weight nobody else noticed. This isn't a sympathy reflex, it's a justice reflex — viewers want a role to root for, not just an outcome to watch.

The synthesis: WAG's emotional promise is **honest stakes, real reactions, and a fair outcome** — including the outcome being "we genuinely don't know." That last part is not a hedge; it's the thing no elite creator in this dataset does as a named, repeated device, and it's WAG's own real, evidenced practice already. Keep it.

## 3. What are our 3-5 long-term content lanes?

**Corrected 2026-08-10: two, not three.** The prior version of this document named a third lane (a "Loyalty Lane" built around fairness/friendship dynamics) and treated it as equal in status to the other two. That was a mistake in kind, not just degree — that lane's actual concepts skewed social/interpersonal, closer to how WAG Podcast content works than how WAG Main content works, and it quietly smuggled in an assumption (that the sisters' relationship is itself a *lane*) that the evidence doesn't support. The sisters' dynamic is a real cast asset — see Question 4 — but an asset is an ingredient a lane can use, not a lane by itself.

**Lane 1 — The Verification Chassis** *(proven, WAG's own evidence, 3/3 videos: cryptid investigation, restaurant review, viral-food testing).* State a real, falsifiable claim in the first 30-45 seconds. Test it honestly. Resolve it in the order it was raised — including "we don't know" as a legitimate ending. This is what `WAG_CONTENT_BUCKETS.md`'s three real buckets (Viral-Testing, Worst-Rated Places, Overnight Cryptid) already are, correctly understood as one lane wearing three costumes, not three lanes.

**Lane 2 — The Real-Stakes Commitment** *(hypothesis — cross-creator's single strongest universal mechanism, but genuinely untested by WAG specifically).* A stated extreme duration or quantity, scored on screen, resolved at a real finish line — the shape behind five of Ryan Trahan's six all-time-top videos across five completely unrelated subjects (airlines, VR, Minecraft, hotels, sensory deprivation), and MrBeast's entire swappable-template titling system. Zero brand-fit conflict, cheap to pilot, worth a real WAG test — but stated as a hypothesis, not a proven lane, and Greenlight does not get to treat it as a requirement (see `WAG_VIDEO_GREENLIGHT.md` Gate 0's Path A/Path B split).

**Explicitly not a third lane, kept open on purpose:** a third lane doesn't get named in advance of evidence. `WAG_VIDEO_GREENLIGHT.md` now has a real Breakout/New Format path — a genuinely new idea can be tested and, if it earns it through real outcomes, become WAG's third lane. That's a different and more honest process than inventing a third lane now to round the number out. `WAG_CONTENT_BUCKETS.md` also already flags Travel/Event Vlogs as real but not a growth bucket — nothing here changes that call.

**Update, 2026-08-10 — this is what earning a refinement through real evidence looks like.** The competitor/algorithm research pass (`WAG_MAIN_COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md`, `WAG_ALGORITHM_DIAGNOSTIC.md`) surfaced two real, evidence-backed refinements — not a third lane invented to round out a number, but genuine sharpening of the two lanes already here:

- **Legend/Overnight Investigation is a real, production-distinct subtype of Lane 1**, not a separate lane. WAG's own #2 all-time post-pivot performer (the Haunted Hotel video, 171K views) is this exact shape, and it's the entire identity of Sam and Colby (16.2M subs — the closest structural match to WAG's own mechanism found in the whole competitor set: state a claim, test it on camera, resolve it). Called out separately because its production requirements genuinely differ (night shoot, location scouting, equipment) — the underlying mechanism is still the Verification Chassis.
- **Category Sweep Completionism ("EVERY X") is a real, named container — but it is not a standalone type.** It's WAG's single most-proven structure by raw numbers (the literal shape of both the #1 and #3 all-time post-pivot performers), but the flopped TikTok Product videos prove the container alone doesn't work — it needs Lane 1's verification wrapper or Lane 2's stakes wrapper around it to land. Neither of these is a new Greenlight requirement — they're refinements to how Lane 1 gets executed, filed under the same honesty discipline as everything else here.

## 4. What repeatable format becomes WAG's IP?

**Corrected 2026-08-10: it doesn't exist yet, and calling honesty "the IP" overstates a brand principle into a claimed asset.** "We don't know" as a legitimate ending is real, it's WAG's own current practice, and no elite creator studied names it as a device — that's genuinely differentiated. But an IP-worthy repeatable *format* has to be something an audience recognizes and returns for on its own, tested across more than one lane, in more than one video. Right now it's been observed in exactly 3 WAG videos, all inside Lane 1. That's a real, evidenced **brand/storytelling principle** — keep it in Question 5's philosophy, apply it everywhere — but it is not yet a discovered, proven format, and this document shouldn't claim more than that.

**What would actually earn the "WAG's repeatable IP" label:** the honesty principle carried deliberately into a second lane (Lane 2's first real pilot) and shown to still work — recognized, returned-to, structurally repeatable outside the genre it was found in. Until that real test happens, the honest answer to this question is: **we have a strong candidate principle, not yet a discovered format.** Answering "what's WAG's repeatable IP" with real confidence is itself one of the open items this document can't close — see the closing section.

## 5. What storytelling philosophy defines every episode?

1. **State the real stake as an explicit, checkable claim within the first 30-60 seconds** — no greeting, no preamble. This is the single strongest cross-creator finding in the entire dataset (5/5 elite creators, independently sourced).
2. **Externally track the stake** — a rule, a countdown, a scoreboard, a claim to be proven or disproven — something the viewer can follow along and score in real time (5/5 universal).
3. **Let reactions be real, not staged.** WAG's non-negotiable production constraint (real people, no scripted plot) is also a trust mechanism four of five elite creators independently arrive at through different implementations — this isn't a limitation, it's already doing real work.
4. **Resolve honestly, in the order the stakes were raised** — including "we don't know" as a legitimate, non-manufactured ending. This is WAG's own real practice and the one thing that differentiates it from every creator studied.
5. **Let comedy come from the footage's own tension**, not a scripted break from it — the strongest single audience-engagement signal found in this research.

## 6. What packaging philosophy defines every upload?

**Corrected 2026-08-10 — both rules below were overstated as universal doctrine in the prior version. WAG's own real title/thumbnail data contradicts parts of what was claimed. Corrected against `WAG_PACKAGING_INTELLIGENCE.md`'s actual evidence, not just the elite-creator research.**

**Titles:** declarative/quantified is the strongest *default* — the packaging-layer expression of the universal cold-open finding, true across all five elite creators studied. But "never a question" is not a real WAG rule: `WAG_PACKAGING_INTELLIGENCE.md`'s own Rule-of-3-confirmed pattern (WAG's "Dating Red Flags," plus LOL Podcast and Rock Pod titles) shows unresolved-question framing genuinely works, via a different, real mechanism — the Zeigarnik effect (unresolved questions are cognitively stickier than resolved statements). The honest rule: **declarative-and-quantified is the default for Lane 1's claim-verification content; a real, unresolved question is the right call when the actual hook is ambiguity, not a claim to test.** Use whichever creates the sharper real information gap for the specific content — not a blanket ban on questions.

**Thumbnails:** **corrected — the prior "honest-photographic over exaggerated-expression" rule was asserted from elite-creator research alone and contradicted by WAG's own real, live performance data.** `WAG_PATTERN_LIBRARY.md`'s corrected finding: WAG's own real highest-performing recent video ("I Ate at EVERY WORST Rated Restaurant In a New City!," 454,560 views) uses an **open-mouthed, genuinely disgusted/alarmed expression** — the opposite of restrained. The same doc found this mirrors FaZe Rug's real top-performing thumbnails and directly contradicts a closed-mouth-always-wins theory once floated from MrBeast's data alone. The honest, corrected rule: **the real distinction isn't restrained-vs-exaggerated, it's genuine-vs-staged.** A real, unrestrained, authentic reaction (open-mouthed disgust, real shock) is exactly what WAG's own best-performing thumbnail already is, and it should not be avoided. The actual guardrail — carried over correctly from the WAG-fit review — is narrower than "keep it subtle": **never a forced or exaggerated expression staged for the thumbnail rather than pulled from a real moment**, which matters specifically because two of the three cast are minors (see Question 9). Genuine intensity is fine; manufactured intensity isn't.

## 7. What makes someone instantly recognize a WAG video?

The real cast, a stated claim within the first minute, a trackable stake, and — the part nobody else in this research does — the willingness to end on an honest "we don't know" rather than a manufactured resolution.

## 8. What makes them come back next week?

The chassis repeats (same trusted structure, new real subject) — the exact device Ryan Trahan and MrBeast both build entire catalogs on. Layered onto that: the safe-fear-plus-long-horizon-comfort pattern this research found repeated across five unrelated channels ("2 years later and still binge watching," "under one hour gang" as a returning-viewer identity marker) — familiarity and trust compound the same way a person you trust does, not the way a trending topic does.

**Already-settled, not new findings from this pass:** horse-content-era material, new slime content. Both remain off-limits.

**Preventive guardrails, named before they become problems, not after — corrected 2026-08-10 to the real family/age facts (`WAG_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md`):** the real cast is Angelina (18) and Annabella (16), who are sisters, plus their cousin Scarlett (17) — not three sisters. Two of the three are minors; Angelina is not. Corrected guardrails:
- Don't let Lane 2's scoreboard/stakes mechanic drift into engineered rivalry between cast members without separately vetting that as its own decision. **Correction, checked against the real channel catalog (2026-08-10, Studio):** this isn't uncharted territory — WAG has repeatedly published real, live videos with explicit team/rivalry framing between cast members ("Are You Team Bella or Team Angelina & Scarlett?!", "a rivalry that's bigger than the horses themselves," "Angelina, Annabella, and Scarlett battled it out"), mostly pre-pivot. The guardrail still stands going forward given the current cast's real ages, but it's a real, already-normalized part of WAG's own history, not a hypothetical risk being introduced for the first time.
- **`WAG_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md`'s own explicit standing rule applies here directly: what creates genuine conflict or support between the three of them is not evidenced and is not this project's call to invent — it's Katie's, and the girls'.** Any concept assuming a specific real interpersonal dynamic beyond the three publicly-cited roles (Angelina plans/steadies, Scarlett talks/needles, Annabella reacts/commits) is speculation, not evidence, until Katie or the girls confirm it directly.
- Don't let thumbnail packaging drift into forced or staged expressions pulled for effect rather than a real moment — matters most for Scarlett and Annabella specifically, since they're minors and Angelina is not, though the same standard should apply to all three as a matter of consistent practice.
- Don't let the honest-resolution principle (Question 4) harden into "find claims to manufacture content" — content has to come from something WAG genuinely did, never backward from a keyword or format gap.
