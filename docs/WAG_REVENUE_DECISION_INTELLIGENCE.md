## 0. Corrected standard (2026-08-12) — applied throughout this document

Katie's correction, applied retroactively to everything below: **GoPro's Grom Quest was surfaced as WAG's top Outbound Opportunity Intelligence proof, but it's a submission-based contest/creator program, not a paid partnership** — see the correction now recorded in `WAG_REVENUE_RECOVERY_PASS_FINDINGS.md` §15. Contests, sweepstakes, free-product/gear programs, generic affiliate programs, and creator challenges do not compete with real paid-partnership prospects in the executive queue going forward. They can be logged as relationship-building leads if genuinely valuable, but they are not the target output.

**Two frameworks now standing for all Revenue Opportunity Intelligence work, not just this document:**

**The full funnel (extends the original ladder, 2026-08-12):** `Market signal → Prospect → Research → Qualified prospect → Executive card → Katie decision → Outreach → Response → Negotiation → Deal/No deal → Outcome → Learning`. Two stages worth naming explicitly: **Executive card** sits between Qualified Prospect and Katie's decision — it's the point where research becomes a decision-ready artifact (§1 below defines the exact format), not before. **Outcome → Learning** closes the loop — every card's prediction gets checked against what actually happened, per §8 below. Discovery does not stop because a candidate reaches Executive Card — the funnel has multiple items in different stages simultaneously, and background pipeline research keeps running in parallel with card-building, not sequentially before or after it.

**The Commercial Opportunity Quality test** — before any prospect is elevated toward Katie as a serious proactive target, evaluate at minimum: commercial evidence (do they actually pay creators?), audience fit, brand safety, creative fit (can this become good WAG content, not a forced ad?), property fit (Main/Podcast/Combined), timing (why now), budget probability (evidence of real spend), access path (an actual person/agency/route in), strategic upside (one-off vs. relationship potential), rights/risk (exclusivity, age, data/privacy, reputation). **No manufactured numerical score** — where evidence doesn't exist, the field stays marked UNKNOWN, not defaulted to a plausible-looking number.

**TIMING — added 2026-08-13 as a first-class dimension, not an afterthought.** A great company at the wrong moment is only a prospect; a strong-fit company with a real launch/campaign event in the next 30-90 days can become an actual opportunity. Every card and watchlist entry from here forward captures, where knowable: launch date, new product/game/release, seasonal campaign window (back-to-school, holiday, summer/travel), creator-casting activity, agency appointment, marketing hiring, or funding/expansion relevant to spend. The goal, stated exactly as Katie framed it: eventually be able to say "this company has always fit WAG, but NOW is the moment to approach them" — not just "this company fits WAG."

**The WATCHLIST — five real states, so nothing valuable gets lost and nothing clutters Katie's queue prematurely:**
- **ACTIVE OPPORTUNITY** — has an executive card, ready for a Katie decision now.
- **QUALIFIED PROSPECT** — real fit + real commercial pathway confirmed, not yet card-ready (needs a stronger access path, a timing signal, or a package decision first).
- **TIMING WATCH** — good fit, wrong moment; recheck on a real future date tied to a real event (a launch, a season, a campaign window).
- **CATEGORY WATCH** — a category-level signal (e.g., repeated AI or gaming inbound) worth continued monitoring, not yet a specific company decision.
- **AGENCY/RELATIONSHIP WATCH** — a person or agency worth tracking over time, independent of any single current deal.

Every watchlist entry carries a reason and a recheck date — never open-ended. **First entry: Sanrio Party Land → TIMING WATCH, recheck ~August 2026 (pre-launch, once a title-specific creator program is announced ahead of the Fall 2026 release) — not elevated to an active opportunity now, exactly because no program exists yet to actually pursue.**

No auto-approval, no exceptions. This is a direct extension of the Katie-in-Control Revenue Operating Rule (Consolidated §37) to contracts generally, not a new authority model.

### 6c. Signature/status tracking

Tracks where an agreement actually is in its lifecycle — drafted, with counsel, sent for signature, countersigned, fully executed, or in dispute. This is the piece most likely to currently be informal/untracked (per the real, evidenced Kidoodle case, where WAG's own certainty about its licensing terms was less complete than assumed) — making this explicit is itself risk reduction, independent of any new deal.

### 6d. Rights ledger

The most structurally important piece, and the one WAG doesn't have today even informally: a real, queryable record of **which rights are granted to whom, for what content, for how long, exclusive or not** — separate from the agreement documents themselves. This is what would have made Epic's rate immediately, structurally un-confusable with market pricing (it already is, per §36, but the ledger is what enforces that going forward instead of relying on institutional memory) and what makes a future licensing deal's exclusivity terms checkable against every other live agreement before signing — e.g., confirming a new FAST/CTV deal doesn't accidentally conflict with an existing Kidoodle or Epic exclusivity clause.

### 6e. Expirations/renewals

A simple, real operational need: nothing currently tracks when Kidoodle's or Epic's terms are up for renewal, or when a future sponsorship's usage-rights window closes. This should surface as a standing "Needs Katie" item on a real calendar basis (e.g., 90/30 days before expiration), not be discovered reactively.

### 6f. Obligations tracking

What WAG owes, not just what WAG is owed — deliverable deadlines, usage restrictions WAG itself agreed to, any exclusivity WAG committed to. Distinct from the AR/Collections procedure (§7 of Prepared Intelligence), which tracks money owed *to* WAG.

### 6g. Usage-right restrictions

Structured, not prose — per-agreement flags for what's allowed: organic-only vs. paid-media, platform restrictions, territory restrictions, time-boxed usage windows. Directly needed for §3's usage-rights pricing gap to ever get resolved — you can't price a right you're not tracking.

### 6h. AI-use/training restrictions

**A genuinely new, current (2026) category** — flagged directly by this pass's own research (pocket.watch began letting creators opt in to license content to AI-training firms as of Jul 2025). WAG needs an explicit position, tracked per agreement, on whether any given deal or distribution relationship grants AI-training rights — this should default to **not granted unless explicitly negotiated**, consistent with WAG's broader caution around AI-authority boundaries elsewhere in WAG OS, and should be a required field on every new agreement going forward, not an afterthought.

### 6i. Escalation to counsel

Named triggers, not a vague "ask a lawyer sometime": any agreement involving the girls' likeness/voice specifically, any deal with AI-training-rights implications, any dispute or non-payment beyond the standing Collections/AR escalation point, and any agreement type from the "most urgent" tier in the existing Legal/Commercial inventory (sponsorship agreements, content licensing, guest releases, likeness/voice rights) before first real use — these get flagged for counsel review, not drafted by WAG OS.

**What this is not:** a legal document generator, a source of legal advice, or a replacement for actual counsel review. It's the operational scaffolding that makes "what's signed, what's owed, what's expiring, what needs a lawyer" answerable at a glance instead of reconstructed from memory each time — the same problem the Revenue schema already solved for deals, applied to the contracts underneath them.
