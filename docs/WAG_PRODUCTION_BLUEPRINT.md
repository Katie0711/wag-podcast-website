# WAG Production Blueprint

**One executive question this module answers: How should a WAG video actually get made, start to finish?**

**Part of the WAG YouTube Growth Operating System (2026-08-09).** This is the production half of the system — `WAG_STORYTELLING_SYSTEM.md` teaches the narrative shape, `WAG_PATTERN_LIBRARY.md` holds the raw evidence, `WAG_VIDEO_GREENLIGHT.md` decides whether an idea gets filmed. This document is what actually happens on and around a filming day.

**Built from real, cited production intelligence** — not generic advice. Sources: MrBeast's team (a leaked internal production document, multiply corroborated by independent outlets, plus Colin & Samir interview quotes), Ryan Trahan's editor Zach Levet (on the record, The Editing Podcast), Jordan Matter's own quote on Salish Matter's weekly pipeline (Tubefilter), and FaZe Rug's real, documented team structure (job postings, a real quote about protected thumbnail IP). Full evidence tiers for every claim below live in `WAG_PATTERN_LIBRARY.md`'s Production Patterns section.

**The one rule every stage below has to respect:** plan the structure, never the words. This is not WAG's constraint alone — it's the real, evidenced practice of the two biggest creators studied (MrBeast: "we don't fake things"; Salish Matter's team: heavy pre-planning, real reactions within it).

## 1. Pre-Production

- **Pick the bucket + format** from `WAG_CONTENT_BUCKETS.md` and `WAG_STORYTELLING_SYSTEM.md`'s Episode Framework Library.
- **Lock the real variable** honestly (the real product category, the real location, the real legend) — verify it's genuinely current/true before committing a filming day to it.
- **Separate the TOPIC decision from the CONTAINER decision — a new, real, evidenced production discipline.** WAG's own real data shows the "EVERY" title device and the "24-hour real-legend" container both produced 14x-250x view spreads depending on subject matter alone (food vs. product-testing; haunted hotel vs. forest cryptid — see `WAG_PATTERN_LIBRARY.md`'s corrected Completeness Claim and Location-Revisit patterns). Before locking a shoot, run two separate yes/no checks: does this container work, and — independently — does this specific topic have real pull? Never let a working container talk the team into a weak topic.
- **Write the idea into `WAG_VIDEO_GREENLIGHT.md`** and score it before scheduling. An idea that hasn't been scored is an idea that hasn't been pressure-tested.
- **Real evidence behind this step:** MrBeast's team plans 3-4 months ahead on larger stunts and pre-tests physical elements (a real coffin, tested over 10 days, before a burial-endurance video). WAG's scale doesn't need a 3-4 month horizon, but the underlying discipline — test the real physical constraint before filming day — transfers directly.
- **Lock real location access and permits by name, as an owned job, not an afterthought.** FaZe Rug's own current, real hiring document requires the production role to "secure waivers, permits, and necessary permissions for locations." For WAG's real-location legend/investigation format, assign one person to close this loop before call time.

## 2. Planning

- **Build the title and thumbnail concept BEFORE filming, not after.** Real, verified practice from both Ryan Trahan's team (his editor Zach Levet, on record: early thumbnails "gave us guidance on what we'd do for the day") and MrBeast's team (real, named thumbnail lead Chucky Appleby, ~50 concepts generated and up to 20 full variants tested per video, per YouTube's own interview). This isn't packaging as an afterthought — it's a planning tool that tells the whole team what the video is actually about before a single frame is shot.
- **Draft 2-3 real branching contingency routes — never a script.** FaZe Rug's own current hiring document requires the role to "develop multiple creative routes for each project to allow flexibility." Jordan Matter's real, on-record method is the same idea in miniature: "we give Salish scenarios that she then has to complete... she doesn't have to say any preset lines or anything." Producer prep = a short real list of "if this happens, we lean into X; if that happens, we pivot to Y" — never dialogue.
- **Name the real checkpoints** (2-3 real moments the day should hit) — not scripted lines, structural beats. E.g., for Overnight Cryptid: dusk / midnight / dawn.
- **Run a real veto check-in with all three cast members before filming, and again mid-shoot if something changes.** Model directly on Jordan Matter's real, stated system: Salish is excluded from all preproduction planning but holds unilateral veto power at both greenlight ("If she says no, then we won't do it") and mid-shoot ("I really leave it all up to her"). Given WAG's real cast includes two minors, this should be a standing, non-negotiable step — not a courtesy.
- **Plan one genuine-surprise element, and decide in advance who is and isn't briefed on it.** Ryan Trahan's producer Preston coordinated real creator cameos without telling Ryan in advance, so Ryan's real, on-camera reaction was genuine. For WAG: a producer (never the girls) can hold back one specific real reveal so at least one reaction per shoot is guaranteed unscripted. **Guardrail: this must never involve withholding safety-relevant information, and given two of WAG's real cast are minors, the category of surprise (not necessarily its specific content) should have advance sign-off.**

## 3. Hook

- **State the real mission in the first 5-15 seconds — always.** VERIFIED, cross-creator (Ryan Trahan's "Game Plan," MrBeast's "no wasted words" cold-open philosophy). No creator studied in this research builds up slowly to the premise.
- **Real, forensic model — MrBeast's real mansion-video opening, second-by-second:** 0:00 cold open direct to the cast, no logo. 0:05, real caption cut mid-sentence: "Whichever one of you leaves this mansion..." ~0:16, a real confessional cutaway cut mid-sentence again. Each fragment is its own micro-cliffhanger — the cut itself, not a narrated transition, is the hook. See `WAG_PATTERN_LIBRARY.md`'s new Withheld-Resolution Fragment Captions pattern.
- **Don't over-explain.** MrBeast's own real example of hook economy: state the situation, the stakes, and the rule, then move — "It gives you everything you need. No wasted words."

## 4. Filming

- **Capture real reactions. Never re-do one to get a "better" take.** This is the line into scripting — the moment a genuine reaction gets reshot for effect, it stops being real.
- **Let real relationship dynamics play out.** Per the corrected Real-Relationships-Generate-Conflict pattern (`WAG_PATTERN_LIBRARY.md`) — the disagreements, the teasing, the real support between the three of them are the actual content.
- **Get the day's premise stated once, on camera, in the girls' own words — a real "Game Plan" beat.** Trahan's Game Plan segment states the day's goal before showing whether it was met; his editor is explicit it was added specifically "to prevent [videos] from becoming just a daily vlog." Ask, don't script: "What are we actually trying to do today?"
- **Over-capture reaction and confessional cutaways throughout the day, not once.** Both MrBeast's team (the real nameplate-on-first-confessional device) and WAG's own strongest real asset (the open-mouthed, genuinely disgusted restaurant reaction, WAG's #1 recent video) show that a real reaction shot is the payload the whole shoot exists to protect. Get it more than once.
- **Shoot extra real B-roll of the environment/anchor object early** — gives real thumbnail options later instead of whatever's left over at the end of the day.
- **Deliberately capture 3-5 short, standalone moments built for Shorts, same day.** WAG's own real Haunted Hotel long-form already generated 5+ real Shorts spinoffs organically — stop treating that as luck. Assign someone on the day to flag "that's a Short" the moment it happens.

## 5. Story Beats

Follow the seven-beat invisible structure from `WAG_STORYTELLING_SYSTEM.md`: Hook -> Container -> Escalation -> Real Stakes -> Reversal/Risk -> Emotional Beat -> Honest Payoff. Plan checkpoints for each beat before filming — never plan the dialogue that happens at each one.

## 6. Producer Prompts (how to guide without scripting)

The real, evidenced technique across every creator studied is a **structured decision point, not a passive hope for a good moment.** Real, specific prompts to use in the moment — never scripted answers:

| Moment | Prompt | Why (real source) |
|---|---|---|
| Right before the cold open is filmed | "Just tell me where we are and why it matters, like you're telling a friend who has no idea what's about to happen." | Produces a word-minimal, stakes-first opening without scripting exact words — MrBeast's own stated cold-open doctrine. |
| Start of the day | "What are we actually trying to do today — in your own words?" | Elicits a real, unscripted Game Plan statement — Ryan Trahan's team built this mechanic specifically to avoid the footage reading as "just a vlog." |
| Immediately before any shoot begins, and again if a plan changes | "Are you actually comfortable with this, or should we skip or change it?" | Directly mirrors Jordan Matter's real, stated two-point veto system for Salish — given WAG's real minors in cast, this should be asked, not assumed. |
| Right after something goes off-plan on camera | "Forget the camera for a second — what do we actually do now?" | Invites a genuine problem-solving reaction, the same real-constraint logic behind Ryan Trahan's Wheel of Doom rules, which the hosts must visibly and actually obey. |
| Just after a big reaction moment | (to the camera operator) "Hold on their face — don't cut yet." | Protects the raw reaction shot as the asset — echoes both MrBeast's confessional-cutaway device and WAG's own real highest-performing thumbnail. |
| Right after a strong, quotable reaction | "Say what just happened in your own words, for someone who wasn't here." | This is how Salish Matter's real, timestamped "pink sugar" comment-bait moment gets generated — a genuine restatement, not a scripted punchline. |
| At the very end, once the stated goal has been resolved | "Did we actually do what we said we'd do at the start?" | Forces a real, on-camera resolution beat that closes the loop opened by the Game Plan/cold-open statement. |

- Open, non-leading prompts work better than closed ones: "what do you think is really out here" rather than "aren't you scared right now" — the first invites a real, unpredictable answer; the second fishes for a performance.

## 7. Escalation

Apply the Stair-Stepping pattern (`WAG_PATTERN_LIBRARY.md`): plan for stakes to visibly increase at 2-3 real points during filming, not stay flat. **Honest scope note, 2026-08-09:** the specific claim that a *mid-single-video* re-engagement beat is broadly cross-validated overstated the evidence — this Red Team-caught pass found it VERIFIED for MrBeast specifically, not 4/5 creators as earlier drafts implied (cross-video franchise escalation, e.g. Airrack's Strangers-to-Celebrities swap, is a mechanically different thing). Treat a planned mid-video beat as a real, single-creator hypothesis worth testing on WAG's own longer videos — log it as an explicit entry in `WAG_EXPERIMENT_REGISTRY.md`, don't fold it into default practice unlogged.

## 8. Ending

- **A real emotional beat, never a stated lesson or moral.** Matches WAG's own existing Stories calibration standard, now cross-validated by the creator research: no format studied ends on an explicit "here's what we learned."
- **The payoff has to honestly match what the hook promised.** The single most common way a good-sounding idea fails is here — real evidence, multiple sources, that overselling in the hook and underdelivering in the ending breaks trust fastest.
- **Keep real outcomes real.** Jordan Matter's stated rule: prizes given to guests are real and kept — "There's a certain degree of authenticity in their reactions." Whatever the real stakes of a shoot are, let the outcome actually stand.

## 9. Review

- **Find the single strongest real hook-worthy moment** — it's usually not the chronological start of the footage, and it belongs in the first 15 seconds of the edit regardless of when it actually happened.
- **Check the real payoff against the real hook's promise** before locking the edit.
- **Review footage as a team quickly, same day or next day.** Ryan Trahan's real model: "Ryan and I would review it, we'd watch it as a team, we'd post it." Fast review keeps authenticity intact and prevents overthinking a real moment into something staged-feeling in the edit.
- **Let the raw footage stay raw where time is short — don't over-produce out of anxiety.** Trahan's editor deliberately kept edits "a lot more minimal" under compressed turnaround and reports the audience responded specifically to content that felt "naturally what it is."

## 10. Packaging

- **Pick the thumbnail from real captured expressions** — never a staged reshoot just for the thumbnail.
- **Match the title to a real framework** from `WAG_PATTERN_LIBRARY.md` / `WAG_PACKAGING_INTELLIGENCE.md` — and independently verify the real topic has demand before trusting the title device alone (see the corrected Completeness Claim pattern).
- **Test both light and dark mode** on the final thumbnail — Ryan Trahan's own documented practice (he's caught and fixed poor dark-mode contrast before).
- **Keep sponsor content in clean, separate description blocks.** MrBeast's own real, live description structure runs two full separate sponsor paragraphs back-to-back rather than weaving sponsor content into narration — see `WAG_PATTERN_LIBRARY.md`'s Sponsor-Integration pattern.

## 11. Publishing

Run the video through `CHANNEL_PUBLISHING_QA_CHECKLIST.md`'s 9-item standard before it goes live — end screen, cards, chapters, playlists, descriptions, pinned comment, website links, playlist links, consistent CTAs.

- **Pin a real, specific comment and/or ask a direct content-preference question.** This is already a real, working WAG tactic (confirmed on the Bigfoot Forest and Horse Owners videos) — do it deliberately every time, and log what's asked so it becomes real audience-research data over time.

## 12. Postmortem

- **Log the real performance data into the same `WAG_VIDEO_GREENLIGHT.md` record** the idea started in — never a separate document. Views, CTR, retention, comments, real numbers.
- **Log topic, container, runtime, and real outcome as separate fields, every time.** WAG's own real confounds this pass uncovered (does runtime suppress reach, or have new videos just not aged? does the container or the topic drive a title pattern's success?) exist specifically because outcomes weren't tracked as separable variables before now. See `WAG_MEASUREMENT_STANDARDS.md`.
- **Write the real lesson, honestly** — what worked, what didn't, what surprised the team. This is what feeds `WAG_PATTERN_LIBRARY.md`, `WAG_STORYTELLING_SYSTEM.md`, and `WAG_PACKAGING_INTELLIGENCE.md` over time, per the Learning Engine loop.
