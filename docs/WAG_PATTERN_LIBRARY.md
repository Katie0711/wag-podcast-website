# WAG Pattern Library

**One executive question this module answers: Will people click, watch, and come back?**

**Part of the WAG YouTube Growth Operating System (started 2026-08-09).** Reverse-engineered from real, cited, evidence-tiered research on MrBeast, Ryan Trahan, Airrack, FaZe Rug, and Jordan/Salish Matter — Packaging & Systems Intelligence sources, not competitors (LOL Podcast and Rock Pod remain the real podcast competitors, per `COMPETITIVE_INTELLIGENCE.md`). This is the evidence layer underneath `WAG_STORYTELLING_SYSTEM.md`, `WAG_PACKAGING_INTELLIGENCE.md`, and `WAG_PRODUCTION_BLUEPRINT.md` — those docs draw conclusions; this one is the raw, structured pattern catalog they draw from.

**Companion HTML module:** a browsable/filterable/searchable version of this same dataset lives at [link once published] — same data, built to be clicked into and compared, not read top to bottom. This markdown file is the canonical source; the HTML is a derived view.

**Evidence discipline, explicit and non-negotiable:** every entry below is labeled VERIFIED (a direct, citable quote/statement from the creator or their team), STRONG EVIDENCE (multiple independent credible sources agree, or one primary source describing it indirectly), OBSERVED PATTERN (found in real title/data, but the mechanism isn't confirmed by anyone), or HYPOTHESIS (a flagged, reasonable inference — never presented as fact). No entry claims to describe "actual dialogue" or footage — the research behind this library came from real public interviews, podcasts, production breakdowns, and press coverage, not from watching raw video, and is cited accordingly.

## Title Patterns

### Pattern: Specific Number + Extreme Constraint — **DEEPENED 2026-08-09, forensic**
- **Evidence tier:** VERIFIED (Ryan Trahan, Colin & Samir Show transcript; MrBeast, leaked production memo, legitimacy confirmed by two former producers per multiple outlets) / STRONG EVIDENCE (cross-creator, real title corpora)
- **Creators:** Ryan Trahan, MrBeast, FaZe Rug, Airrack
- **Real examples:** "I Survived On $0.01 For 30 Days" (Trahan), "$1 vs $100,000,000 Car!" (MrBeast), "I Survived 24 Hours WITHOUT Touching the Ground!" (FaZe Rug)
- **Description:** a hyper-precise number ("$0.01," not "a penny") paired with an extreme, named constraint.
- **The forensic mechanism, verbatim from MrBeast's own leaked production memo:** the memo's own worked example contrasts "I Spent 50 Hours In Ketchup" against "I Spent 50 Hours In My Front Yard" for equivalent production effort — the specific, visualizable, faintly absurd noun (ketchup) outperforms the vague one (front yard) because it gives the viewer a concrete mental image to click toward. The same document separates "Bananas Are The Worst Food On Earth" (a falsifiable, arguable, specific claim) from "I Don't Like Bananas" (a flat, low-stakes statement) — the lesson is specificity of the *noun*, not just the number.
- **Real, forensic Ryan Trahan title corpus (directly captured, dozens of entries, not a sample):** "I Tested Every Level of Airplane Food," "I Took the Longest Train Ride in America," "I Tested Every 1-Star Hotel," "I Stayed in Every State's Top Ranked Airbnb." Every one follows [first-person past-tense verb] + [exhaustive/superlative qualifier: Every/Longest/Most Expensive/1-Star] + [category noun]. The extremity word IS the hook — it states literally, factually, completely what the video promises to show, which only works if the constraint is real and verifiably completed on camera. This is a real, falsifiability-forcing mechanism: a title that says "Every 1-Star Hotel" is checkable in a way "You Won't Believe This Hotel" is not.
- **Real, cross-confirmed sub-pattern — one load-bearing capitalized word per title, exactly one:** independently observed in both Airrack's and FaZe Rug's real, current title corpora. Airrack: DISCONTINUED, BANNED, EVERY, Abandoned — the rest of the sentence stays plain case. FaZe Rug: identical discipline (his real titles use exactly one capitalized modifier). Strip the one word and the premise collapses — "I Bought Items From Our Childhood" is not a video; "I Bought DISCONTINUED Items" is. The modifier isn't decoration, it IS the premise. This is a real, portable, cheap production discipline distinct from the number-precision half of this pattern.
- **Psychology:** precision bias — specific numbers and nouns read as more truthful/less exaggerated than round or vague ones, lowering skepticism before the click; a single capitalized word functions as the one place the eye is meant to snag mid-scroll.
- **Strengths:** cold-audience-friendly (no cast knowledge required); the number/noun itself is the whole hook.
- **Weaknesses:** the number has to survive being checked — a rounded-up or exaggerated number breaks trust immediately.
- **When to use:** any format built on a real, checkable constraint.
- **When NOT to use:** when the real constraint can't be stated precisely without exaggeration.
- **WAG adaptation:** direct fit, zero production-model conflict. Already validated once (see below). The one-capitalized-word discipline is a real, cheap, immediately-testable refinement worth applying to every future WAG title, independent of any bigger format change.
- **Comparable WAG example:** "We Survived On Only $1 Snacks for a Week" (Greenlight Round 1, scored 12/14).

### Pattern: Completeness Claim ("EVERY ___")
- **Evidence tier:** STRONG EVIDENCE (two independent creators) + **CORRECTED 2026-08-09 — see below**
- **Creators:** FaZe Rug, Airrack, **WAG itself**
- **Real examples:** "I Bought Every BANNED Item in America!" / "I Bought EVERY Abandoned Product I Could Find" (FaZe Rug, real, live-viewed titles), "How Many Days Can I Secretly Live In ___?" (Airrack — completeness-of-attempt variant)
- **Description:** claims exhaustiveness over a category — every store, every product, every day possible.
- **Psychology:** implies a countdown/checklist the viewer wants resolved, plus a completeness itch.
- **CORRECTION, 2026-08-09 — this pattern was previously overclaimed as validated WAG prior art. It is not, on its own.** A real, live Studio pull found four real WAG "EVERY" titles, not three, with a **250x real view spread using the identical device**: "I Ate at EVERY WORST Rated Restaurant In a New City!" (454,560 views) and "I Ate EVERY Viral TikTok Food In a New City!" (374,459 views) vs. "I Tested EVERY Viral TikTok Product I Could Buy!" (1,732 views) and "Trying EVERY Viral TikTok Product On the Internet!" (1,693 views — **the exact video this pattern previously cited by name as proof; it is actually one of the channel's weakest recent uploads, not confirming evidence**). The two weak titles were also published just 10 days apart, a real, likely self-cannibalizing near-duplication.
- **What actually separates the winners from the losers, read from the real data:** topic, not the "EVERY" device. Food/restaurant "EVERY" claims cleared 374K-454K views; product-testing "EVERY" claims cleared under 2K. A real, observed third-party keyword-score tool (vidIQ) scored the two weak videos 25/100 ("Very low volume") vs. 50/100 for a real strong performer in a different pattern — directional, not proof, but consistent with a real search-demand explanation: nobody searches for "viral TikTok product tests" the way they search for restaurant/food content.
- **Strengths:** the device is necessary but demonstrably not sufficient — it works when wrapped around genuinely searched-for, high-interest topics.
- **Weaknesses:** breaks trust fast if "EVERY" isn't close to true; **also now evidenced to fail outright when the underlying topic has weak real demand, regardless of title craft.**
- **When to use:** any real, bounded category with independently verified real search/interest demand — check the topic before trusting the title device.
- **When NOT to use:** when true exhaustiveness isn't achievable in one video, **or when the topic itself hasn't been checked for real demand** — don't assume the device transfers just because it worked once on a different topic.
- **WAG adaptation:** revise `WAG_PACKAGING_INTELLIGENCE.md`'s "When NOT to use" field for this pattern to add this exact correction. Real production discipline: separate the topic decision from the container decision before locking a shoot — two independent checks, not one.
- **Comparable WAG example:** the real, corrected pair — "EVERY WORST Rated Restaurant" (454K, real win) vs. "Trying EVERY Viral TikTok Product" (1.7K, real underperformer) — same device, same channel, same year, a 250x gap. Source: WAG Intelligence dossier, live Studio pull, 2026-08-09.

### Pattern: Trapped / Confined Container
- **Evidence tier:** VERIFIED (real titles) / STRONG EVIDENCE (production process for the Airrack instance)
- **Creators:** Airrack, FaZe Rug
- **Real examples:** "I Trapped 25 Strangers In A House" (Airrack — team explicitly reverse-engineered *Big Brother's* pacing for this one), "Trapped in a Lamborghini for 24 Hours" (FaZe Rug)
- **Description:** a real, bounded physical space + a real time limit, with people (not necessarily strangers) confined inside it.
- **Psychology:** claustrophobia/escalation + forced-proximity social dynamics.
- **Strengths:** doesn't require inventing conflict — confinement itself generates real friction over time.
- **Weaknesses:** Airrack's "strangers in a house" instance needed real casting, insurance, and a crew WAG's small team doesn't have — the *scale* of that specific instance isn't portable.
- **When to use:** small-scale version with people who already have real relationships (not strangers) — sidesteps the casting/insurance problem entirely.
- **When NOT to use:** don't attempt a stranger-casting version without real production infrastructure.
- **WAG adaptation:** genuinely usable at small scale — "trapped" doesn't require strangers; three real sisters in one real confined space with a real time limit works with zero additional casting.

### Pattern: Location-Revisit / Owned Mystery Place
- **Evidence tier:** STRONG EVIDENCE (Rug's own real tunnel franchise, independently re-verified 2026-08-09 — see below) + **CORRECTED for WAG's own comparable example**
- **Creators:** FaZe Rug (the Miramar "Haunted Tunnel," San Diego)
- **Description:** not a single-video title pattern — a *franchise* pattern. One real, legally accessible location gets revisited across years, becoming an owned, recognizable "mystery place" the audience associates specifically with that creator.
- **Real, re-verified evidence for FaZe Rug specifically:** a real, dated sequence of tunnel entries runs from the earliest located instance (~9 years old, 5M views, YouTube's own auto-summary: "FaZe Rug and a cousin enter a dark, rainy tunnel") through real escalation beats (a Pokemon GO crossover, "WE GOT ATTACKED... *POLICE CALLED*") to a real crossover with an independently-known paranormal-duo, Sam & Colby (~4 years old, 19M views — the highest-viewed tunnel entry found). **Independent external validation:** a real, unrelated 2024 mini-series episode is titled "Boys R Us Visit FaZe Rug's Haunted Tunnel at 3AM" on IMDb — a third party using "FaZe Rug's Haunted Tunnel" as the location's proper name years later, unprompted. This is genuinely strong evidence the mechanism works — for Rug.
- **CORRECTION, 2026-08-09 — the WAG comparable example previously cited here does not actually support the pattern.** A real Studio pull found a **14x real view gap** between the two "cross-linked" WAG videos: "24 Hours in America's Most Haunted Hotel!" (171,354 views) vs. "24 Hours in America's Most Bigfoot Sighted Forest!" (11,761 views) — and a second forest-cryptid entry, Goatman's, independently confirms the same low range (11,954 views). Within an **identical container** (24-hour real-legend format), the gap tracks subject matter (domestic/haunted-hotel horror vs. forest/cryptid horror), not repetition or cross-linking. The organic end-screen cross-linking is real, but the data does not show it "compounding value" the way the franchise-pattern theory implies — it shows one subject category (haunted/domestic) reliably outperforming another (forest cryptid) by a wide, repeated (n=2) margin. Mechanism unconfirmed by any source; flagged as OBSERVED PATTERN, not explained.
- **Psychology:** for Rug's real, verified case — repeated exposure to the same real place builds a sense of ongoing mystery/mythology that a new location every time can't. **For WAG's data specifically, this framing is not yet supported** — the real driver observed so far is subject matter, not revisit-and-compound value.
- **Strengths:** requires zero incremental budget after the first visit — the asset is repetition, not spend (true for Rug's real case).
- **Weaknesses:** takes years to pay off; not a quick win. **WAG-specific: don't assume revisiting a location will itself lift performance — the real data suggests picking the right subject matter (domestic/haunted vs. forest/cryptid) matters more than repetition alone, at least so far.**
- **When to use:** pick ONE real, legally accessible, genuinely atmospheric location and commit to revisiting it deliberately, not accidentally — once real evidence (not just organic end-screen linking) shows it's paying off.
- **When NOT to use:** don't force a "mystery place" narrative onto a location that isn't real to the format, and don't treat organic cross-linking alone as proof the franchise mechanism is working.
- **WAG adaptation:** the real, corrected finding — subject matter (haunted/domestic horror) appears to be the dominant variable within WAG's own "24-hour real-legend" container, not location-revisit itself. Worth a deliberate WAG-side test: a 3rd real haunted/domestic-horror entry vs. a 3rd forest-cryptid entry, tracked as a genuine experiment (see `WAG_EXPERIMENT_REGISTRY.md`), before treating either theory as settled.
- **Comparable WAG example:** the real, corrected pair — Haunted Hotel (171K) vs. Bigfoot Forest (11.7K) vs. Goatman's Forest (11.9K) — same container, real 14x-15x gap, subject matter as the likely (unconfirmed) driver. Source: WAG Intelligence dossier, live Studio pull, 2026-08-09.

## Hook Patterns

### Pattern: The Game Plan / Mission Statement, Stated Fast
- **Evidence tier:** VERIFIED (Ryan Trahan's editor, on the record) + VERIFIED (MrBeast, Colin & Samir transcript)
- **Creators:** Ryan Trahan, MrBeast
- **Real examples/quotes:** Trahan's editor Zach Levet: the "Game Plan" segment "developed naturally... became a critical part of every episode." MrBeast, describing a real cold open: "I tied up an FBI agent. I have $100,000 in this bag. Here's the knife, good luck... It gives you everything you need. No wasted words." MrBeast, on his own obsession: "constantly obsessed with mostly the first minute."
- **Description:** state the real rules/stakes/mission almost immediately — not built up to, not teased, just stated.
- **Psychology:** removes ambiguity fast so curiosity attaches to outcome, not to "what is this video even about."
- **Strengths:** zero production cost, purely a discipline of what gets said/shown first.
- **Weaknesses:** none identified — this pattern has no real evidenced downside across sources.
- **When to use:** every video, no exceptions found in the evidence.
- **When NOT to use:** no real counter-evidence found.
- **WAG adaptation:** direct fit, already partially reflected in `WAG_STORYTELLING_SYSTEM.md`'s Hook beat — this research strengthens it from "reasonable idea" to VERIFIED, cross-creator practice.
- **Comparable WAG example:** the Overnight Cryptid format's existing hook practice (state the real legend + plan immediately).

### Pattern: Hook Externalized to Title/Thumbnail (not verbal cold-open)
- **Evidence tier:** OBSERVED PATTERN (no direct interview confirmation)
- **Creators:** FaZe Rug
- **Description:** for some creators, the "hook" work happens entirely in the title/thumbnail (which states the stakes/prize/rule), rather than a spoken cold-open inside the video.
- **Psychology:** the click itself is the commitment; the video doesn't need to re-sell the premise once someone's already clicked.
- **Strengths:** simpler production (no need to engineer a verbal hook).
- **Weaknesses:** relies entirely on packaging quality — a weak title/thumbnail has no in-video backup.
- **When to use:** when the packaging is already doing real, honest premise-stating work.
- **When NOT to use:** as a substitute for a weak title rather than a complement to a strong one.
- **WAG adaptation:** worth testing but not over-indexing on — WAG's own retention data (from the earlier project phase) shows real, specific below-typical intro-retention problems on at least one video, suggesting in-video hook discipline still matters for WAG even if some creators can skip it.

### Pattern: Withheld-Resolution Fragment Captions — **NEW, forensic, 2026-08-09**
- **Evidence tier:** VERIFIED (directly viewed, real MrBeast opening, second-by-second)
- **Creators:** MrBeast
- **Real, forensic sequence (real, live-viewed opening of "Last To Leave Mansion, Keeps It," Aug 2026):** 0:00 cold open, straight to the blindfolded cast on the driveway, no logo, no narrated setup. 0:05, real caption cuts mid-sentence: "Whichever one of you leaves this mansion..." — the sentence is never completed on screen. ~0:09, real caption over the cast running through the front door: "wahooOOOoO!! Just so you understand" — tone-setting, not exposition. ~0:16, a real confessional cutaway cuts mid-sentence again: "How are they expecting me to go back to my..." Each fragment is its own micro-cliffhanger; the cut itself, not a spoken transition, is the hook.
- **Description:** captions are deliberately cut off mid-sentence at the exact moment of maximum curiosity, forcing the viewer to keep watching for the other half of a sentence that may never be completed verbatim.
- **The mechanism, connected directly to MrBeast's own stated doctrine:** "It gives you everything you need. No wasted words" (his description of a different real cold open, FBI-agent premise) — the fragment-caption device is the same "no wasted words" discipline applied at the sentence level: each fragment supplies the premise's shape without spending the runtime finishing the sentence.
- **Psychology:** stretches engagement inside the first minute (where MrBeast states he is "constantly obsessed") without adding runtime — each fragment is a separate, small curiosity gap rather than one long build-up.
- **Strengths:** zero production cost — a caption/edit discipline, not a new asset.
- **Weaknesses:** only works if the fragment genuinely implies a real, coming answer — an empty tease reads as manipulative if the payoff never lands.
- **When to use:** any real cold open with a genuine multi-part premise (a rule, a stake, a real reaction) that can be split across 2-3 real beats.
- **When NOT to use:** don't fragment a sentence that has no real second half — the device depends on the resolution actually existing.
- **WAG adaptation:** direct, cheap, immediately testable — apply to the next real cold open by deliberately cutting the caption/statement at its most information-dense point rather than completing it in one breath.
- **Comparable WAG example:** none yet — a real, cheap experiment to run.

## Thumbnail Patterns

### Pattern: One Clean Anchor, Restrained Expression
- **Evidence tier:** VERIFIED (YouTube Creator Blog interview)
- **Creators:** Ryan Trahan
- **Real example/quote:** his own stated bar, "Did we make this with love?" — team took 3,000 photos over 5 pre-dawn mornings for one usable frame; explicitly rejects "over-the-top clickbait faces" for subtler expression.
- **Description:** one real visual anchor object, real (not exaggerated) facial expression, natural lighting, tested for dark-mode visibility.
- **Psychology:** credibility signal — restraint reads as "this is real" the way exaggeration reads as "this might be fake."
- **Strengths:** builds long-term trust, which compounds CTR over time per the same logic MrBeast independently confirms below.
- **Weaknesses:** may underperform against exaggerated thumbnails in a pure novelty-driven feed — this is a real, evidenced trade-off, not a free win.
- **When to use:** when the real content genuinely matches a restrained, credible tone.
- **When NOT to use:** N/A — no evidence this fails anywhere it's been tried.
- **WAG adaptation:** direct fit — matches WAG's own no-fabrication content standard closely.

### Pattern: Closed-Mouth Beats Open-Shocked (A/B Tested) — **CORRECTED: genre-dependent, not universal**
- **Evidence tier:** VERIFIED for MrBeast's own channel (real, disclosed Sept 6 2023 X posts, corroborated by multiple secondary sources) — **but DIRECTLY CONTRADICTED by two independent real data points found 2026-08-09; this is no longer a "default to it" rule.**
- **Creators:** MrBeast (source), FaZe Rug and WAG itself (real counter-evidence)
- **Real finding, MrBeast:** across roughly 30 consecutive real videos, thumbnails with a closed-mouth expression outperformed open-mouth/shocked-face versions on watch time in every test — MrBeast's own words, and his team's stated mechanism is Face-Trust Consistency (see below): the closed-mouth smile banks trust from a prior video's payoff onto the next click decision.
- **Real counter-evidence, found this pass:** FaZe Rug's real, live, currently high-performing thumbnail ("I Bought DISCONTINUED ITEMS From Our Childhood," directly viewed) uses an open-mouthed, wide-eyed "shocked-delighted" reaction face as the composition's focal point. Separately and independently, **WAG's own real, highest-performing recent long-form video** ("I Ate at EVERY WORST Rated Restaurant In a New City!", 454,560 views — WAG's strongest recent asset) uses an open-mouthed, genuinely disgusted/alarmed expression — the exact opposite of the closed-mouth rule.
- **The corrected, honest read:** not resolved by any single source, and this pass can't resolve it either — flagged as a real, unresolved tension rather than papered over. The likeliest explanation, unconfirmed: the mechanism is genre-dependent. Dare/stunt/authority content (MrBeast's core format) rewards restraint because it's banking trust from a prior video's payoff. Food-disgust/reaction content (WAG's and FaZe Rug's real winners) rewards a visibly authentic, unrestrained reaction as proof the moment is real. A third possibility, also unconfirmed: MrBeast's own real "DAY 29" thumbnail (mock-distressed, not smiling, opposite a warmly smiling elderly woman) shows even his own channel doesn't apply "smile" as a fixed rule — the real operating principle may be "match the expression to what makes the specific footage credible," and closed-mouth was simply the frequent right answer under Face-Trust Consistency until it wasn't.
- **Psychology:** for MrBeast's format specifically, likely the same credibility/trust mechanism as Ryan Trahan's restraint pattern. For WAG's and FaZe Rug's real winners, more likely a different mechanism — visible, unrestrained reaction as proof-of-authenticity for reaction-driven content.
- **Strengths:** the original finding is real and directly useful for dare/stunt-format content.
- **Weaknesses:** **do not treat this as a universal rule — WAG's own best-performing real asset directly falsifies it as a blanket default.**
- **When to use:** dare/stunt/authority-driven content, where the thumbnail's job is banking trust from a prior video.
- **When NOT to use:** food/product-reaction content, where WAG's and FaZe Rug's real evidence suggests an unrestrained, genuine reaction may perform better — don't default to restraint here without testing.
- **WAG adaptation:** treat expression choice as a genre-specific, testable variable, not a fixed rule. See `WAG_EXPERIMENT_REGISTRY.md`, Experiment 1, for the real WAG A/B test this should run as.
- **Comparable WAG example:** "I Ate at EVERY WORST Rated Restaurant In a New City!" (454,560 views) — real, open-mouthed, genuinely disgusted expression, WAG's own strongest recent counter-example. Source: WAG Intelligence dossier, 2026-08-09.

- **Strengths:** directly reinforces WAG's own content-authenticity standard as a real, evidenced growth strategy, not just an ethical nicety.

### Pattern: Arrow-and-Label Puzzle Grammar
- **Evidence tier:** VERIFIED (directly viewed and zoomed, 4 separate real thumbnails, 2026-08-09)
- **Creators:** Airrack
- **Real examples:** "I Snuck Into The White House" (bold white "me" + red hand-drawn arrow pointing at himself among real Secret Service agents), "I 'Cheated' Until the Arcade Banned Me" (19M views — same red arrow, now pointing at a hidden lens in his glasses), a second "Cheating" thumbnail at a casino roulette table (same device), "Last To Get Kicked Out Of The Grocery Store Wins" (face camouflaged into a pile of watermelons, arrow marking the hidden face).
- **Description:** one thick, hand-drawn-weight red arrow plus one bold, marker-scrawled label, applied consistently across otherwise completely different visual environments (arcade neon, casino felt, produce aisle, White House colonnade). The arrow's job shifts per thumbnail — sometimes answering "which of these people is Airrack" (a spot-the-subject puzzle), sometimes "how is he doing this" (a spot-the-mechanism puzzle) — but the grammar itself never changes.
- **Psychology:** turns the thumbnail into a puzzle with exactly one visual answer, forcing a beat of active looking rather than passive scanning — the label supplies the question, the arrow supplies where to look for the answer.
- **Strengths:** genuinely portable across any environment (the background palette changes to match real lighting each time; the reusable asset is the puzzle grammar, not a fixed color scheme). Cheap to execute — one arrow, one label, no complex compositing beyond the hidden detail itself.
- **Weaknesses:** depends on a real hidden or incriminating detail existing in the frame — doesn't work if there's nothing genuinely worth pointing at.
- **When to use:** any real thumbnail built around a genuine "spot the thing" moment (a hidden object, an ambiguous identity, a real mechanism).
- **When NOT to use:** don't force an arrow onto a thumbnail with no real puzzle to solve — it reads as noise, not a device.
- **WAG adaptation:** a real, low-cost, testable device for any WAG thumbnail built around a genuine reveal or hidden detail (e.g., a real disguised location, a real hidden reaction). Worth a direct test rather than assumed transfer.
- **Comparable WAG example:** none yet — a real, cheap experiment to run.

### Pattern: Density-as-Pitch (Product-Pile-as-Payoff)
