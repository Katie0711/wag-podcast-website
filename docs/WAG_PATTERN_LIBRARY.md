# WAG Pattern Library

**One executive question this module answers: Will people click, watch, and come back?**

**Part of the WAG YouTube Growth Operating System (started 2026-08-09).** Reverse-engineered from real, cited, evidence-tiered research on MrBeast, Ryan Trahan, Airrack, FaZe Rug, and Jordan/Salish Matter — Packaging & Systems Intelligence sources, not competitors (LOL Podcast and Rock Pod remain the real podcast competitors, per `COMPETITIVE_INTELLIGENCE.md`). This is the evidence layer underneath `WAG_STORYTELLING_SYSTEM.md`, `WAG_PACKAGING_INTELLIGENCE.md`, and `WAG_PRODUCTION_BLUEPRINT.md` — those docs draw conclusions; this one is the raw, structured pattern catalog they draw from.

**Companion HTML module:** a browsable/filterable/searchable version of this same dataset lives at [link once published] — same data, built to be clicked into and compared, not read top to bottom. This markdown file is the canonical source; the HTML is a derived view.

**Evidence discipline, explicit and non-negotiable:** every entry below is labeled VERIFIED (a direct, citable quote/statement from the creator or their team), STRONG EVIDENCE (multiple independent credible sources agree, or one primary source describing it indirectly), OBSERVED PATTERN (found in real title/data, but the mechanism isn't confirmed by anyone), or HYPOTHESIS (a flagged, reasonable inference — never presented as fact). No entry claims to describe "actual dialogue" or footage — the research behind this library came from real public interviews, podcasts, production breakdowns, and press coverage, not from watching raw video, and is cited accordingly.

## Title Patterns

### Pattern: Specific Number + Extreme Constraint
- **Evidence tier:** VERIFIED (Ryan Trahan, Colin & Samir Show transcript) / STRONG EVIDENCE (cross-creator)
- **Creators:** Ryan Trahan, MrBeast, FaZe Rug
- **Real examples:** "I Survived On $0.01 For 30 Days" (Trahan), "$1 vs $100,000,000 Car!" (MrBeast), "I Survived 24 Hours WITHOUT Touching the Ground!" (FaZe Rug)
- **Description:** a hyper-precise number ("$0.01," not "a penny") paired with an extreme, named constraint.
- **Psychology:** precision bias — specific numbers read as more truthful/less exaggerated than round ones, lowering skepticism before the click.
- **Strengths:** cold-audience-friendly (no cast knowledge required); the number itself is the whole hook.
- **Weaknesses:** the number has to survive being checked — a rounded-up or exaggerated number breaks trust immediately.
- **When to use:** any format built on a real, checkable constraint.
- **When NOT to use:** when the real constraint can't be stated precisely without exaggeration.
- **WAG adaptation:** direct fit, zero production-model conflict. Already validated once (see below).
- **Comparable WAG example:** "We Survived On Only $1 Snacks for a Week" (Greenlight Round 1, scored 12/14).

### Pattern: Completeness Claim ("EVERY ___")
- **Evidence tier:** STRONG EVIDENCE (two independent creators) + VERIFIED (WAG's own real prior art)
- **Creators:** FaZe Rug, Airrack, **WAG itself**
- **Real examples:** "I Tested Every Store Open AFTER Midnight" (FaZe Rug), "How Many Days Can I Secretly Live In ___?" (Airrack — completeness-of-attempt variant), **"Trying EVERY Viral TikTok Product On the Internet!" (real, published WAG video)**
- **Description:** claims exhaustiveness over a category — every store, every product, every day possible.
- **Psychology:** implies a countdown/checklist the viewer wants resolved, plus a completeness itch.
- **Strengths:** WAG already has real, working prior art here — this is a confirm-and-extend pattern, not a new bet.
- **Weaknesses:** breaks trust fast if "EVERY" isn't close to true.
- **When to use:** any real, bounded category (products, places, rules).
- **When NOT to use:** when true exhaustiveness isn't achievable in one video — don't fake completeness.
- **WAG adaptation:** already proven. Keep sourcing the real category honestly at filming time (per `WAG_CONTENT_BUCKETS.md`'s idea generator).
- **Comparable WAG example:** the 3 real WAG videos already using this pattern (see `WAG_CONTENT_BUCKETS.md`, Viral-Testing bucket).

### Pattern: Extreme Comparison ($1 vs. $X)
- **Evidence tier:** STRONG EVIDENCE (real title data + Forbes/creator-economy coverage)
- **Creators:** MrBeast
- **Real examples:** "$1 vs $500,000 Plane Ticket!", "$1 vs $1,000,000,000 Yacht!"
- **Description:** the same category shown at two extreme price/scale points.
- **Psychology:** curiosity gap between the two poles; doesn't require narrative, just contrast.
- **Strengths:** extremely cold-audience-friendly, purely visual/conceptual.
- **Weaknesses:** requires real budget scale to execute honestly at MrBeast's level — the mechanic (not the dollar amounts) is what's portable.
- **When to use:** any real category with a meaningful cheap/expensive real-world range.
- **When NOT to use:** if WAG can't fund a real, honest "extreme" end — a fake or trivial "extreme" undercuts the whole format.
- **WAG adaptation:** scale down the dollar range to something real and honestly extreme *for WAG's actual budget* (e.g., "$1 vs $50 Viral Skincare" rather than pretending to MrBeast's scale) — the contrast principle transfers, the absolute numbers don't need to.
- **Comparable WAG example:** none yet — a real gap.

### Pattern: Named-Relationship Stakes
- **Evidence tier:** OBSERVED PATTERN (title data) + STRONG EVIDENCE (production process, Jordan Matter's own quote)
- **Creators:** Salish Matter
- **Real examples:** "Who Knows Me Better? *Best Friend vs Dad*", "Salish Matter Controls My Life for 24 Hours"
- **Description:** the title names a real, existing relationship as the entire stake — no invented plot needed.
- **Psychology:** parasocial investment in a real, ongoing relationship the audience already has context for.
- **Strengths:** this is WAG's single biggest natural advantage — three real sisters with a real, ongoing relationship, which no creator studied in this library has as a built-in asset the way WAG does.
- **Weaknesses:** needs a real, willing second participant (friend/family) every time — a real logistics dependency, not a content weakness.
- **When to use:** whenever the pairing is genuinely real and willing.
- **When NOT to use:** never manufacture a fake relationship dynamic to fit this pattern — breaks content-authenticity.
- **WAG adaptation:** direct, high-priority fit. See Episode Framework Library — "Who Knows [Sister] Better?"
- **Comparable WAG example:** "Who Knows Annabella Better: Her Sisters or Her Best Friend?" (Greenlight Round 1, scored 12/14).

### Pattern: Trapped / Confined Container
- **Evidence tier:** VERIFIED (real titles) / STRONG EVIDENCE (production process for the Airrack instance)
- **Creators:** Airrack, FaZe Rug
- **Real examples:** "I Trapped 25 Strangers In A House" (Airrack — team explicitly reverse-engineered *Big Brother's* pacing for this one), "Trapped in a Lamborghini for 24 Hours" (FaZe Rug)
- **Description:** a real, bounded physical space + a real time limit, with people (not necessarily strangers) confined inside it.
- **Psychology:** claustrophobia/escalation + forced-proximity social dynamics.
- **Strengths:** doesn't require inventing conflict — confinement itself generates real friction over time.
- **Weaknesses:** Airrack's "strangers in a house" instance needed real casting, insurance, and a crew WAG's small team doesn't have — the *scale* of that specific instance isn't portable.
- **When to use:** small-scale version with people who already have real relationships (not strangers) — sidesteps the casting/insurance problem entirely.
- **When NOT to use:** don't attempt a stranger-casting version without real production infrastructure.
- **WAG adaptation:** genuinely usable at small scale — "trapped" doesn't require strangers; three real sisters in one real confined space with a real time limit works with zero additional casting.
- **Comparable WAG example:** none yet — a real, buildable gap.

### Pattern: Location-Revisit / Owned Mystery Place
- **Evidence tier:** STRONG EVIDENCE (Rug's own 2017 tweet + years of repeat uploads)
- **Creators:** FaZe Rug (the Miramar "Haunted Tunnel," San Diego)
- **Description:** not a single-video title pattern — a *franchise* pattern. One real, legally accessible location gets revisited across years, becoming an owned, recognizable "mystery place" the audience associates specifically with that creator.
- **Psychology:** repeated exposure to the same real place builds a sense of ongoing mystery/mythology that a new location every time can't — audience investment compounds instead of resetting.
- **Strengths:** requires zero incremental budget after the first visit — the asset is repetition, not spend.
- **Weaknesses:** takes years to pay off; not a quick win.
- **When to use:** pick ONE real, legally accessible, genuinely atmospheric location and commit to revisiting it deliberately, not accidentally.
- **When NOT to use:** don't force a "mystery place" narrative onto a location that isn't real to the format.
- **WAG adaptation:** directly relevant — WAG's own end-screen data already shows an *organic*, undesigned version of this (the Bigfoot and Haunted Hotel videos cross-link to each other in end screens, unprompted). Worth naming deliberately rather than leaving accidental. See WAG DNA section below.
- **Comparable WAG example:** the real cross-linking pattern between "24 Hours in America's Most Bigfoot Sighted Forest!" and "24 Hours in America's Most Haunted Hotel!" — already happening, not yet named as a deliberate franchise.

## Hook Patterns

### Pattern: The Game Plan / Mission Statement, Stated Fast
- **Evidence tier:** VERIFIED (Ryan Trahan's editor, on the record) + VERIFIED (MrBeast, Colin & Samir transcript)
- **Creators:** Ryan Trahan, MrBeast
- **Real examples/quotes:** Trahan's editor Zach Levet: the "Game Plan" segment "developed naturally... became a critical part of every episode." MrBeast, describing a real cold open: "I tied up an FBI agent. I have $100,000 in this bag. Here's the knife, good luck... It gives you everything you need. No wasted words." MrBeast, on his own obsession: "constantly obsessed with mostly the first minute."
- **Description:** state the real rules/stakes/mission almost immediately — not built up to, not teased, just stated.
- **Psychology:** removes ambiguity fast so curiosity attaches to outcome, not to "what is this video even about."
- **Strengths:** zero production cost, purely a discipline of what gets said/shown first.
- **Weaknesses:** none identified — this pattern has no real evidenced downside across sources.
- **When to use:** every video, no exceptions found in the evidence.
- **When NOT to use:** no real counter-evidence found.
- **WAG adaptation:** direct fit, already partially reflected in `WAG_STORYTELLING_SYSTEM.md`'s Hook beat — this research strengthens it from "reasonable idea" to VERIFIED, cross-creator practice.
- **Comparable WAG example:** the Overnight Cryptid format's existing hook practice (state the real legend + plan immediately).

### Pattern: Hook Externalized to Title/Thumbnail (not verbal cold-open)
- **Evidence tier:** OBSERVED PATTERN (no direct interview confirmation)
- **Creators:** FaZe Rug
- **Description:** for some creators, the "hook" work happens entirely in the title/thumbnail (which states the stakes/prize/rule), rather than a spoken cold-open inside the video.
- **Psychology:** the click itself is the commitment; the video doesn't need to re-sell the premise once someone's already clicked.
- **Strengths:** simpler production (no need to engineer a verbal hook).
- **Weaknesses:** relies entirely on packaging quality — a weak title/thumbnail has no in-video backup.
- **When to use:** when the packaging is already doing real, honest premise-stating work.
- **When NOT to use:** as a substitute for a weak title rather than a complement to a strong one.
- **WAG adaptation:** worth testing but not over-indexing on — WAG's own retention data (from the earlier project phase) shows real, specific below-typical intro-retention problems on at least one video, suggesting in-video hook discipline still matters for WAG even if some creators can skip it.

## Thumbnail Patterns

### Pattern: One Clean Anchor, Restrained Expression
- **Evidence tier:** VERIFIED (YouTube Creator Blog interview)
- **Creators:** Ryan Trahan
- **Real example/quote:** his own stated bar, "Did we make this with love?" — team took 3,000 photos over 5 pre-dawn mornings for one usable frame; explicitly rejects "over-the-top clickbait faces" for subtler expression.
- **Description:** one real visual anchor object, real (not exaggerated) facial expression, natural lighting, tested for dark-mode visibility.
- **Psychology:** credibility signal — restraint reads as "this is real" the way exaggeration reads as "this might be fake."
- **Strengths:** builds long-term trust, which compounds CTR over time per the same logic MrBeast independently confirms below.
- **Weaknesses:** may underperform against exaggerated thumbnails in a pure novelty-driven feed — this is a real, evidenced trade-off, not a free win.
- **When to use:** when the real content genuinely matches a restrained, credible tone.
- **When NOT to use:** N/A — no evidence this fails anywhere it's been tried.
- **WAG adaptation:** direct fit — matches WAG's own no-fabrication content standard closely.

### Pattern: Closed-Mouth Beats Open-Shocked (A/B Tested)
- **Evidence tier:** STRONG EVIDENCE (real, disclosed A/B test across ~30 videos, corroborated by multiple secondary sources; exact primary quote wording not independently re-verified)
- **Creators:** MrBeast
- **Real finding:** across roughly 30 consecutive videos, thumbnails with a closed-mouth expression outperformed open-mouth/shocked-face versions on watch time in every single test.
- **Description:** the "exaggerated shock face" that's treated as YouTube-thumbnail conventional wisdom actually underperformed a calmer, closed-mouth expression, real and repeated evidence.
- **Psychology:** likely the same credibility/trust mechanism as Ryan Trahan's restraint pattern above — two creators, independently, landing on the same real finding.
- **Strengths:** directly, meaningfully contradicts generic "always use a shocked face" advice — this is exactly the kind of counter-intuitive, evidence-backed finding this whole research pass was commissioned to surface instead of recycled advice.
- **Weaknesses:** one team's real A/B result — not independently re-run by WAG yet.
- **When to use:** the real evidence says: default to it.
- **When NOT to use:** the honest caveat — this hasn't been tested on WAG's own audience specifically.
- **WAG adaptation:** a genuinely testable, low-cost recommendation — worth a real WAG A/B test on the next few thumbnails rather than assumed.
- **Comparable WAG example:** none yet — a real, cheap experiment to run.

### Pattern: Face-Trust Consistency
- **Evidence tier:** STRONG EVIDENCE (Forbes, direct quotes from MrBeast's thumbnail lead)
- **Creators:** MrBeast
- **Real quote:** "If you trusted Jimmy on the last video... you'd click on this video as well." Team's own stated logic: "If we had lied in any of our videos, people felt misled, the audience wouldn't keep building."
- **Description:** the creator's face appears in nearly every thumbnail, deliberately, as a trust signal built over time — and the team's own stated reasoning is that thumbnails must never misrepresent content.
- **Psychology:** trust compounds across uploads; a single misleading thumbnail is described (by MrBeast's own team) as damaging to every future click, not just that one video.
- **Strengths:** directly reinforces WAG's own content-authenticity standard as a real, evidenced growth strategy, not just an ethical nicety.
- **Weaknesses:** none identified.
- **When to use:** always — treat every thumbnail's promise as a real commitment.
- **When NOT to use:** N/A.
- **WAG adaptation:** direct fit. This is the single strongest piece of evidence in this whole library that WAG's existing no-fabrication rule is also the correct growth strategy, not a constraint on one.

### Pattern: Bright/High-Energy/Shock Aesthetic
- **Evidence tier:** STRONG EVIDENCE (one detailed independent source, real characterization)
- **Creators:** Salish Matter
- **Real quote (third-party, Screenwise):** "the human embodiment of a YouTube thumbnail — high-energy, perfectly lit, and perpetually surrounded by bright colors."
- **Description:** the direct opposite of Ryan Trahan's restraint pattern — bright, high-energy, "shock" expression as the default.
- **Psychology:** likely optimized for a younger, more visually-saturated feed context than Trahan's audience.
- **Strengths:** real evidence this works for her real audience and scale.
- **Weaknesses:** directly contradicts the restraint pattern above — **this library does NOT resolve that contradiction, and neither should WAG pretend one universal thumbnail truth exists.** Different creators, different real audiences, different real results.
- **When to use / when not to use:** this is exactly why WAG needs its own real A/B data (see Closed-Mouth pattern above) rather than importing either extreme wholesale.
- **WAG adaptation:** flag as an open, unresolved question — worth WAG's own real testing, not a default to copy from either direction.

## Story / Escalation Patterns

### Pattern: Stair-Stepping Escalation
- **Evidence tier:** STRONG EVIDENCE (leaked internal production document, multiply corroborated, not personally confirmed by MrBeast on the record)
- **Creators:** MrBeast
- **Description:** progressive stakes increase in discrete steps (e.g., $1 → $100,000) rather than a flat premise or a single jump.
- **Psychology:** each step re-baselines the viewer's sense of "how far will this go," resetting curiosity repeatedly within one video.
- **Strengths:** directly matches WAG's own already-recommended checkpoint pacing (see `WAG_STORYTELLING_SYSTEM.md`'s Overnight Cryptid example) — this research adds a second, independent piece of real evidence for that same structural instinct.
- **Weaknesses:** requires the real content to actually escalate — can't be faked into a flat premise after the fact.
- **When to use:** any format with a real, buildable middle section.
- **When NOT to use:** thin, one-beat premises that can't sustain real escalation.
- **WAG adaptation:** direct fit — apply explicitly to the Overnight Cryptid checkpoint structure and any new "Trapped" format.

### Pattern: Manufactured-by-the-Rules Reversal Device
- **Evidence tier:** VERIFIED (Ryan Trahan's own "Wheel of Doom," documented mechanism)
- **Creators:** Ryan Trahan
- **Real example:** a physical spinning wheel with 50 real penalty/twist outcomes, triggered at every $50,000 donation milestone in 50 States in 50 Days.
- **Description:** tension comes from a real, pre-built rule (a wheel, a reset mechanism) that generates a real, unpredictable consequence — not a scripted plot twist.
- **Psychology:** variable-reward suspense, the audience knows a real consequence is coming but not which one.
- **Strengths:** honest — the outcome is genuinely unknown even to the creator, satisfying the no-fabrication standard by construction, not just by discipline.
- **Weaknesses:** needs a real, pre-committed rule-set designed before filming.
- **When to use:** any format that can build in a real random/triggered consequence.
- **When NOT to use:** if the "randomness" isn't actually real (see the Fixable Round 1 idea, "We Let TikTok Decide," flagged for exactly this reason).
- **WAG adaptation:** directly reinforces the existing Round 1 finding — this is now a named, evidenced pattern, not just a hunch.

### Pattern: Mid-Video Re-engagement Beat
- **Evidence tier:** VERIFIED (MrBeast, Colin & Samir transcript) + VERIFIED (MrBeast's stated 70% retention benchmark)
- **Creators:** MrBeast
- **Real quote:** "If you're doing a longer video, you should introduce like a side story... some plan halfway through... to re-engage them so they don't just get bored." Stated benchmark: "If he doesn't get 70% retention on a video, then he didn't do something right."
- **Description:** a deliberate structural beat planted partway through a longer video specifically to prevent a mid-video attention drop.
- **Psychology:** counters the natural retention decay curve every video has.
- **Strengths:** a concrete, plannable production step, not just "make it good."
- **Weaknesses:** requires real mid-video content, not a repeated recap.
- **When to use:** any video over roughly 8-10 minutes.
- **When NOT to use:** very short-format content where the whole video already functions as one beat.
- **WAG adaptation:** direct fit for WAG's longer overnight-challenge and testing videos — plan one specific mid-video escalation deliberately, not just hope pacing holds.

### Pattern: Real Relationships Generate Conflict, Never Scripted Plot
- **Evidence tier:** STRONG EVIDENCE (Salish Matter's team, two independent third-party sources) + VERIFIED (MrBeast, "we don't fake things")
- **Creators:** Salish Matter, MrBeast (independently convergent)
- **Description:** the single most important cross-creator finding in this whole library. Two very different creators, at very different scales, independently land on the same model: plan the *structure* heavily in advance (Salish's Mon–Sat prep pipeline; MrBeast's leaked minute-by-minute retention plan), but the *reactions within that structure* are real, not scripted.
- **Psychology:** audiences can tell the difference between a real reaction to a real situation and a performed line — this is likely *why* both creators, independently, insist on it.
- **Strengths:** this is not a constraint WAG is uniquely imposing on itself — it's the actual, evidenced practice of two of the biggest names studied.
- **Weaknesses:** requires real advance planning discipline (the structure) even while refusing to script the content (the words) — it is real production work, not "just wing it."
- **When to use:** always, per WAG's own standing rule — now cross-validated, not just self-imposed.
- **When NOT to use:** N/A.
- **WAG adaptation:** this IS WAG's existing model (`WAG_STORYTELLING_SYSTEM.md`'s "plan the shape, never the words"). Treat this as the single strongest validating finding of the whole research pass, worth stating explicitly to the girls: **you're not the outlier for refusing to script — MrBeast and Salish Matter's teams already prove the biggest names don't either.**

## Production Patterns

### Pattern: Package Before You Produce
- **Evidence tier:** VERIFIED (Ryan Trahan's editor) + STRONG EVIDENCE (MrBeast's team, Forbes)
- **Creators:** Ryan Trahan, MrBeast
- **Description:** thumbnail/title concepts are built *before or during* filming, not as an afterthought in the edit — Trahan's editor said early thumbnails "really solidified the project" by giving daily directional guidance; MrBeast's team builds 10-50 thumbnail variants before a video is treated as real.
- **Strengths:** forces the team to know what the real hook/promise is before committing a filming day to it — a real quality-control step.
- **When to use:** every real production.
- **WAG adaptation:** already reflected in `WAG_VIDEO_GREENLIGHT.md`'s per-idea record (Title, Thumbnail concept fields exist before production) — this research confirms that ordering is correct practice, not premature.

### Pattern: Weekly Production Rhythm With a Dedicated Crew Role
- **Evidence tier:** VERIFIED (Jordan Matter's own quote, Tubefilter)
- **Creators:** Salish Matter
- **Real quote:** "I spend time with our team Monday through Saturday, prepping the video for Sunday so that she doesn't have to do much more than arrive and have fun." A family-friend creative director (Rowan) handles directing so Jordan can stay on-camera and preserve the real relationship.
- **Description:** a real, structured weekly cadence — most of the week is planning, one day is filming, and a trusted non-parent (but still family-connected) person directs so the parent/sibling relationship isn't burdened with production authority.
- **Strengths:** directly solves a real, evidenced tension — someone has to direct, but the person directing shouldn't also be the person the talent needs a real relationship with off-camera.
- **WAG adaptation:** genuinely useful structural idea — worth considering whether WAG's own production has (or needs) an equivalent "trusted non-parent director" role, separate from whoever's real relationship with the girls needs to stay unburdened by production authority. Flagged as a real, evidenced idea worth discussing with Katie directly, not a decision made here.
