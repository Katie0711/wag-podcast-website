# WAG Main: Next 10 — Ranked, Evidence-First

**WAG Main only.** No Podcast concepts included (see [[wag_two_site_roles]]). Ranked by evidence/confidence, not by certainty — nothing here is a guarantee, and every entry states what would have to be true for it to fail.

**The engine question governs every ranking decision.** Per `WAG_ALGORITHM_DIAGNOSTIC.md`'s real Studio pull (2026-08-10), WAG has two distinct, already-proven ways to reach non-subscribed viewers, and its one confirmed flop triggered neither:

| Video | Views | What triggered it |
|---|---|---|
| "EVERY WORST Rated Restaurant" (winner) | 460.9K (+267.9K vs. typical) | **Suggested videos 75.9%** — packaging/hook won cold algorithmic testing |
| "24 Hours...Haunted Hotel" (winner) | 171.4K (about typical) | **YouTube search 46.5%** — real, pre-existing search demand pulled viewers in |
| "EVERY Viral TikTok Product" (flop) | 1.7K (-7.9K vs. typical) | Neither — 40% Channel pages (mostly already-loyal viewers, not new discovery) |

Every recommendation below states which of these two engines it's betting on, and why. A concept that can't name a real answer to that question is the same trap the flop fell into — that's the single biggest lesson this whole exercise produced, and it's applied as a hard filter here, not a footnote.

**Top 3 get full treatment** per Katie's spec (title, thumbnail, promise, format, opening, full structure, escalation, payoff, why-click, why-continue, why-YouTube-could-push, cost, confidence, failure conditions, and the real evidence that led to the recommendation). **The remaining 7 get a lighter but still real, evidence-cited treatment** — four exceptional picks fully argued beat ten mediocre ones padded to a round number, so the back seven are intentionally shorter, not weaker.

# TOP 3

## #1 — "I Ate EVERY Item on the Menu at [WAG's Own Worst-Rated Restaurant]!"

**One-sentence viewer promise:** We're going back to the worst-rated restaurant we've ever tested — and this time we're not leaving until we've tried everything on the menu.

**Thumbnail concept:** Genuine wide-eyed/disgusted real reaction (matching the exact expression style already proven on WAG's top performer, not a staged-calm face) + a visible, cropped photo of the actual menu with items checked/crossed off — the checklist-as-thumbnail device proven across DiGiovanni's flag row and SIS vs BRO's logo row.

**Format/story engine:** Completion ("Every X") stacked on Verification — Formula 2.3/3.1 in `WAG_TITLE_FORMULA_LIBRARY.md`. This is a direct sequel to WAG's own #1 post-pivot performer, not a new format.

### The real evidence behind this recommendation
- WAG's own "I Ate at EVERY WORST Rated Restaurant In a New City!" — **460.9K views, 267.9K above WAG's typical range, 75.9% Suggested-videos traffic, 18.1% Browse features** (source: live Studio pull, `WAG_ALGORITHM_DIAGNOSTIC.md`). This is WAG's single strongest, most recent, most directly comparable data point in the entire research base.
- WAG's own "I Ate EVERY Viral TikTok Food In a New City!" — 375K views, same Completion chassis, second-strongest post-pivot performer.
- Counterexample that proves the risk of *not* doing this: WAG's own "EVERY Viral TikTok Product" videos (x2, ~1.7K each) — same container, no verification/stakes wrapper, flopped. The lesson: completion alone doesn't work, completion **stacked with verification** (an actual bad-review claim being tested) is what's proven.

### Opening 30-60 seconds
Cold open on the actual 1-star review that sent WAG back to this specific restaurant — read on camera, verbatim, matching the real device already used in WAG's own restaurant videos and Trahan's "I Tested 1-Star Restaurants" (0:24 rule-statement pattern). State the rule immediately: "Every item, every category on the menu, no skipping the scary ones." Show the real menu on camera as a physical checklist prop.

### Full retention structure
- **0:00-1:00** — cold open on the review, rule stated, menu prop shown as the visual "scope of the claim."
- **1:00-3:00** — arrival, real reactions to the space, first item ordered.
- **3:00-25:00** — category-by-category testing (apps, entrees, sides, drinks, dessert), each category its own mini-beat with a visible checkmark on the menu prop — this is the re-engagement device, giving viewers a reason to keep watching for the next checkmark.
- **~15:00** — mid-video escalation: the item the original bad review specifically warned about, tested with the actual complaint read aloud first (mirrors WAG's own real "texture complaint tested across three bowls of soup" beat).
- **25:00-28:00** — final verdict: does the full menu confirm or contradict the restaurant's bad rating? Real answer, including if the answer is mixed.

### Ending/payoff
An honest, specific verdict — which items were unfairly maligned, which lived up to the bad reviews — not a blanket "it wasn't that bad." WAG's own prior version deliberately broke the confirm-the-reviews pattern on its 4th stop; keep that same willingness to be surprised.

### Why a teen viewer would click
The checklist thumbnail promises a specific, completable scope (not vague "we tried food") plus the built-in curiosity of "worst-rated" — a dare-you-to-watch premise that doesn't require knowing WAG's cast beforehand.

### Why they'd continue watching
Each menu category is its own small cliffhanger with a visible checkmark — the same "how many are left" mechanic that makes DiGiovanni's flag-row videos sustain 37+ minutes.

### Why YouTube could reasonably push this to a larger audience
It's a near-exact repeat of the one video WAG has *directly proven* wins cold Suggested-videos testing (75.9% Suggested on the original). This is the lowest-inference bet in the whole list — it's not a hypothesis about what might work, it's asking the algorithm to do again what it already did once, recently, on this exact channel.

### Production difficulty/cost
Low. One restaurant, one day, no new equipment, no travel, no outside cast — the cheapest video on this entire list.

### Confidence
**High.** Direct sequel to a real, recent, channel-specific winner with real traffic-source proof, not analogical evidence from another creator.

### What would have to be true for this to fail
That audiences read this as a repeat rather than a sequel and don't click a second time on a familiar premise — real risk of "seen this before" fatigue if released too close to the original or without a genuinely different restaurant/twist. Mitigation: pick a restaurant with a meaningfully different bad-review angle (e.g., service complaints vs. the original's food-safety angle) so the "what's this time's version of the twist" curiosity still exists.

## #2 — "24 Hours Testing the Chupacabra Legend! (Is It Really Out There?)"

**One-sentence viewer promise:** We're spending 24 hours in real Chupacabra territory to find out if Texas's most famous cryptid legend is actually real.

**Thumbnail concept:** Genuine nighttime reaction shot (equipment-lit, real fear/curiosity expression) + a small "3 Questions We're Answering" text device, matching WAG's own proven Haunted Hotel thumbnail template exactly.

**Format/story engine:** Verification/Investigation — Formula 2.1 in `WAG_TITLE_FORMULA_LIBRARY.md`, WAG's proven claim-test-resolve chassis (see `WAG_CREATIVE_FORMULA_V1.md` Lane 1).

### The real evidence behind this recommendation
- WAG's own "24 Hours in America's Most Haunted Hotel! \*Ghost in Room\*" — **171.4K views, 46.5% YouTube search traffic, 27.9% Channel pages, 71% retention at 0:30** (above typical) — real, direct proof this exact chassis can trigger the **Search engine**, not just Suggested (source: live Studio pull).
- FaZe Rug, "Our Terrifying Night at World's Most Haunted Museum" — 9M views, same chassis at larger scale, corroborating cross-creator evidence.
- Sam and Colby's entire 16.2M-subscriber channel runs this identical chassis at scale — further corroboration the mechanism generalizes.
- The real counter-evidence that shapes this specific pick: WAG's own "24 HOURS in the GOATMAN'S FOREST!" — only 11K views, a real 15x drop versus Hotel on the identical chassis, four months later. Named in the research as **"the single most important WAG-specific data point"** in the whole investigation body of evidence. The leading (unproven) explanation is that "Goatman"/Lake Worth Monster is a real but narrower, more regional legend with less standing search volume than "haunted hotel"-style queries — Chupacabra is deliberately chosen here as a **more nationally recognized legend name** than Goatman, specifically to test whether search-demand breadth, not the investigation chassis itself, was the real variable. This is explicitly a diagnostic bet, not a guaranteed repeat of Hotel's result.

### Opening 30-60 seconds
Cold open stating the legend as a real, historically reported claim (Chupacabra sightings are real, documented regional folklore, not invented) — mirroring WAG's own "This hotel has been called one of the most haunted hotels in Texas" opening line. State 3 concrete yes/no questions the video will answer, on screen, in the first minute — the same device already proven on Hotel.

### Full retention structure
- **0:00-2:30** — legend setup, real reported history, 3-questions device stated on screen.
- **2:30-5:40** — arrival at real rural South/Central Texas terrain associated with the legend, first physical setup of testing equipment.
- **5:40-10:00** — first equipment activity/ambiguous event, mirroring Hotel's real structure.
- **10:00-20:00** — escalating nighttime testing, a real turning-point event (the specific beat can't be scripted in advance — WAG's honesty-first brand requires this to be genuinely whatever happens on the night, not a pre-written moment).
- **20:00-24:00** — "What Really Happened" resolution, directly answering the 3 opening questions, including "we don't know" as a legitimate answer for any unresolved one.

### Ending/payoff
Same honest-resolution structure as Hotel — a direct, specific answer to each of the 3 stated questions, not a vague "so was it real?" wrap-up.

### Why a teen viewer would click
A nationally-recognizable cryptid name (higher pre-existing awareness than Goatman) plus a concrete, numbered-questions curiosity hook.

### Why they'd continue watching
The 3-questions device gives viewers a specific tally to watch for being answered — the same structural device already proven to hold 71% retention at 0:30 on Hotel.

### Why YouTube could reasonably push this to a larger audience
Betting on the **Search engine**, not Suggested — "Chupacabra" is a real, standing search term independent of WAG's channel, the same real-demand mechanism that pulled 46.5% of Hotel's traffic from YouTube search rather than relying on cold algorithmic testing of the video itself.

### Production difficulty/cost
Medium — overnight shoot, real equipment, two of three cast members present are minors, requires the same safety/logistics planning as WAG's prior overnight shoots (see `WAG_PRODUCTION_BLUEPRINT.md`).

### Confidence
**Medium.** The chassis is proven twice at very different results (Hotel high, Goatman low) — this is a genuine live test of the leading hypothesis (search-term breadth is the real variable), not a repeat of a single proven winner.

### What would have to be true for this to fail
That Chupacabra search demand is actually lower than assumed, or that WAG's specific execution can't out-search or out-package the large volume of existing Chupacabra content already on YouTube from bigger paranormal channels. If this underperforms, it would be real, useful diagnostic evidence either way — worth explicitly tracking as a prediction-vs-actual test per the standing WAG Brain directive.

## #3 — "We Only Ate 1-Star Texas Restaurants For 7 Days Straight"

**One-sentence viewer promise:** Seven days, seven of the worst-rated restaurants in Texas, no cheating and no skipping — can we survive a full week of 1-star food?

**Thumbnail concept:** Day-counter burned into the frame (e.g., "DAY 4") over a genuine reaction shot — direct application of MrBeast's proven day-counter packaging device.

**Format/story engine:** Challenge/Commitment — Formula 1.1 in `WAG_TITLE_FORMULA_LIBRARY.md`. **This is the pilot for WAG's still-unproven "Real-Stakes Commitment" lane** (`WAG_CREATIVE_FORMULA_V1.md` Lane 2, currently a hypothesis with zero real WAG examples) — deliberately built by stacking a mechanism WAG has never used (sustained multi-day commitment) on top of a topic WAG has already proven works (worst-rated restaurants).

### The real evidence behind this recommendation
- Ryan Trahan, "I Survived On $0.01 For 30 Days" — 30M views — proves the core mechanism: a stated tiny/limited resource plus a hard finish line sustains a long-form multi-day video.
- MrBeast, "7 Days Stranded At Sea" (437M) and "I Spent 7 Days In Solitary Confinement" (298M, plus a 50-hour variant at 344M as a real two-entry franchise) — proves the day-counter device and the "difficulty escalates as days pass" structure independently, across two different creators.
- WAG's own "I Ate at EVERY WORST Rated Restaurant In a New City!" (460.9K, Suggested 75.9%) — the topic half of this concept is already WAG's proven #1 performer; this recommendation extends its duration rather than inventing a new topic.
- Named risk in the research: this exact mechanism has drawn real "faked challenge" accusations even against Trahan's full production crew — flagged honestly here, not smoothed over.

### Opening 30-60 seconds
State the rule in the first 20 seconds: 7 real days, one 1-star restaurant per day, no repeats, no skipping a meal. Show a physical 7-day tracker (calendar or checklist prop) as the visual scope device.

### Full retention structure
- **Day 1 (0:00-4:00 of the edited video)** — rule stated, first restaurant, establishes the format viewers will see repeat with variation.
- **Days 2-3** — pace quickens, each day gets shorter screen time than Day 1 now that the format is established, but the difficulty escalates: Day 3's restaurant is deliberately the worst-reviewed of the week, moved up specifically to avoid a flat, un-escalating middle.
- **Day 4 (real midpoint)** — a genuine complication (a canceled reservation, a bad reaction, anything real) that raises the stakes the rest of the week has to answer — the "midpoint change" device already documented in `WAG_STORYTELLING_SYSTEM.md`'s ABC-storyline addition.
- **Days 5-6** — recovery and re-commitment, visible real fatigue as part of the honest stakes.
- **Day 7** — the hardest-rated restaurant of the whole week, saved for last deliberately (unlike Day 3's early escalation, this is the final payoff difficulty).

### Ending/payoff
An honest week-in-review verdict: which day was worst, whether the cast would do it again, real physical/mental cost acknowledged rather than played for pure comedy — matching WAG's honesty-first brand extended across a week instead of one segment.

### Why a teen viewer would click
The day-counter format is instantly legible even to viewers who've never seen a MrBeast or Trahan video — "can they survive the whole week" is a self-explanatory stake.

### Why they'd continue watching
Escalating difficulty (Day 3 twist, Day 4 midpoint complication, Day 7 finale) prevents the "watched one day, seen them all" flatness that a naively repetitive 7-day format risks.

### Why YouTube could reasonably push this to a larger audience
This is the one Top-3 pick that can plausibly trigger **both** engines: the "1-star Texas restaurant" search terms give it a real shot at Search traffic (like Hotel), while the packaging (day-counter, escalating stakes) is built specifically to win cold Suggested testing the way the original Restaurant video did.

### Production difficulty/cost
**Medium-High — the real constraint on this whole idea.** Genuinely unbroken daily footage across 7 real days, with a 3-person no-crew cast, is a real production risk named explicitly in the source research (the same one already flagged against Trahan's own videos). This needs an actual shooting-schedule plan before greenlight, not an assumption that "we'll just film for a week."

### Confidence
**Medium.** The topic (worst-rated restaurants) is WAG's own proven winner; the mechanism (sustained multi-day commitment) is proven only by other creators, never by WAG. This is a genuine pilot, explicitly the test case for whether Lane 2 deserves to exist at all in WAG's formula — a real answer either way is valuable.

### What would have to be true for this to fail
That WAG can't actually deliver 7 real consecutive days of restaurant access/footage without visible production gaps, which would both hurt the video and undercut trust in a "real, unbroken" claim central to WAG's brand. A pre-production logistics check (are 7 willing 1-star restaurants realistically bookable in one week in one metro area?) should happen before this gets scheduled.
