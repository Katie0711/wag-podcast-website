## 5. International + Dubbing Opportunity — real research, two small experiments recommended

Full memo (8 pages) saved at `scratchpad\WAG_International_Dubbing_Research_Memo.pdf` — not the docs/ repo, since this was research-only. Key real findings, condensed:

- **Demand signals:** Spanish (LatAm) is the strongest evidenced market (corroborated by MrBeast, Veritasium, Brave Wilderness real case data). Portuguese/Brazil real and under-served. Hindi/Indian regional languages strong for education-adjacent content. YouTube's 2025 Culture & Trends data shows general challenge/mystery-format demand across Korea, Germany, Mexico, Brazil, Indonesia — **but no direct evidence found for WAG's specific cryptid/mystery-investigation sub-genre abroad specifically; flagged as a real gap, not assumed.**
- **Costs/quality — single most important datapoint:** YouTube's native auto-dub is now free (27 languages, expanded Feb 2026), but **Brave Wilderness measured AI-dub retention at 1:22 average view duration vs. 5:19 for professional human dubbing on the same channel/language — a 4-5x gap.** Professional dubbing runs roughly $5-40+/min depending on tier; some vendors use rev-share instead of upfront fees. Metadata-only translation is free but has no isolated case-study data of its own.
- **Real precedent, both directions:** Real wins exist (Brave Wilderness, Veritasium's 5-year compounding international play). Real cautionary case too: a same-video test found posting to a dedicated foreign-language channel got ~100x the views of just adding a dubbed audio track to the existing channel. Most positive published case studies come from localization vendors with a sales interest in the outcome — success stories are likely over-represented in what's publicly written.
- **Rights — genuinely new considerations, not covered by existing WAG rules:** music sync licenses are often territory-scoped and may not cover dubbed re-release; because WAG Main features real minors, dubbing is a *new* likeness/voice use requiring specific (not blanket) consent — distinct from WAG's existing minors'-privacy rules, which govern location/identity disclosure, not voice licensing. COPPA and the existing Epic!/Kidoodle.TV license terms both need checking before touching the educational catalog specifically.

**Recommendation (explicitly labeled as a recommendation, not a decision):** two small, cheap, reversible experiments, consistent with WAG's standing Experimentation discipline (`WAG_EXPERIMENT_REGISTRY.md`) — (A) free YouTube auto-dub on ~10-15 educational videos in Spanish/Portuguese, measuring retention against the real Brave Wilderness benchmark above; (B) translated-metadata-only test on a few WAG Main videos, audio untouched entirely, which sidesteps the minors-voice-consent question completely. **Explicit non-recommendations:** do not touch licensed titles (Epic!/Kidoodle) or dub the girls' actual voices until contracts/consent are separately confirmed; do not commit to any whole-catalog dubbing based on this memo alone — exactly the standing rule Katie set before this research began.

## 7. Collections/AR Operating Procedure — the approved workflow, formalized

**This is a workflow specification, buildable entirely against the schema already migrated (Recovery Pass Findings §9) — no new tables needed, and no code is being written here, just the procedure itself made explicit.**

```
1. Invoice needed detected
   -- e.g. a recurring licensing period closes (Kidoodle-style), or a sponsorship
      deliverable is confirmed complete
   -- Revenue prepares invoice data: deal_id, amount, period, line items
   -- STATUS: revenue_invoices.status = 'prepared'

2. Katie approval
   -- A revenue_approvals row is created (action_type='send_invoice', status='needs_katie')
   -- Katie reviews the prepared invoice data and either approves or requests changes
   -- STATUS: revenue_invoices.status = 'awaiting_katie_approval' -> 'approved'

3. Invoice sent
   -- Only after Katie's explicit approval -- no automatic transition
   -- STATUS: revenue_invoices.status = 'sent', sent_date recorded

4. Due date tracked
   -- due_date recorded on the invoice (when known -- real evidence, e.g. Kidoodle's
      three real invoices, shows this is often NOT confirmable from email alone;
      the workflow must tolerate a null due_date rather than inventing one)

5. Payment monitored
   -- revenue_payments rows recorded as real payments arrive (never inferred from
      an email saying "we'll pay" -- per the standing rule proven in the real
      Kidoodle case)
   -- revenue_invoice_ar_status view (already live) derives payment_status
      (unpaid/partial/paid) and, only when a real due_date exists, overdue status

6. Reminder / follow-up
   -- A real, evidenced pattern (Kidoodle) shows this is often multiple touches
      over months, not one reminder -- the workflow should expect and record a
      real sequence via revenue_deal_events (event_type=follow_up), not a single
      templated reminder

7. Escalation
   -- When follow-ups don't produce a response, a revenue_deal_events row
      (event_type=collections_action_recommended) records the real recommendation
      -- exactly the pattern already proven on the real Kidoodle case
      (Recovery Pass Findings §14a)

8. Collections recommendation -> Katie decision
   -- A revenue_approvals row (action_type=collections_action, status=needs_katie)
      gates any actual escalation action -- confirmed working end-to-end on the
      real Kidoodle proof
   -- No external action (reminder email, escalation contact, payment plan,
      fee waiver, legal/collections-agency involvement) happens without Katie's
      explicit decision recorded (decided_by, decided_at, decision_notes)
```

**This is not new design — it's the same pattern already proven live on the real Kidoodle case, written out as the standing procedure so future invoices (Epic, any new sponsorship deal) follow the identical, already-tested loop rather than a new one being improvised each time.**
