## 1. Target reader definition

**Reader vs. buyer, kept separate on purpose:**
- **Reader:** a kid roughly 7–12, drawn to hands-on/sensory activities, already engages with slime/experiment content (WAG's own or comparable), reads for "what do I get to *do*," not "what do I get to learn."
- **Buyer:** almost always a parent or gift-giver, evaluating on: does this feel worth the price at a bookstore/Target shelf, does it feel safe/age-appropriate, does it feel like more than a YouTube printout, is it something their kid will actually use more than once.
- **Real anchor point, not invented:** this range is inferred from WAG's own real audience skew and the comp category (activity/experiment books for this age band) — it has not been independently verified against WAG's actual audience demographics. **Flag: real audience age-distribution data has not been pulled this pass.** Cheap way to close this gap: check YouTube Studio's Audience tab age/gender breakdown (real data, five-minute pull) before finalizing trim size/reading level.

## 12. Target-reader testing protocol

Reader and buyer instruments stay separate (different real questions), kept deliberately lean per Katie's own prior correction — small and real beats large and slow:

**Kids (real target-age readers):** What page would you look at first? Which character do you like? What do you want to try? What was confusing? Would you want another one?
**Parents (asked separately, without seeing the kid's reactions first):** Would you buy this? For what age? What price feels reasonable? Does it feel educational enough to justify purchase without feeling like school? Would your child use the QR/digital experience?
**Added this pass, per Katie's review-mining instruction:** after the real test, compare results against what negative reviews of the comp titles (§2) complained about — did the prototype avoid those same complaints, or repeat them.
**Discipline:** observe real behavior, not just what kids say they like — what they actually pick up and do.
