# WAG Legal Documentation Package — Executive Summary

Prepared 2026-08-07 for Katie Swans, The Wild Adventure Girls, Inc. Cover memo for the attorney-review package: this summary, the final Privacy Policy draft, the final Terms of Service draft, and the full Legal Readiness Report.

## What Changed

The live Privacy Policy and Terms of Service (last updated July 27, 2026) were already real, tailored legal documents — not generic templates. This work did not rewrite them. It:

1. **Closed a real gap created by today's launch.** The interaction platform (WAG Verdict, WAG Match, Vote for Your Favorite WAG Segment, Questions Featured, WAG Awards) went live in production today, and neither document mentioned it. Added an "Interactive Features" section to each, describing the real mechanics — anonymous voting, quiz results, free-text submissions, and the specific consent-checkbox system already built and verified in production.
2. **Named every real service specifically.** Beehiiv, Netlify (hosting, Netlify Forms, Netlify Blobs), Google Analytics, Ahrefs Analytics, and local storage are each named precisely instead of only referenced generically.
3. **Added a formal "not a sweepstakes or contest" disclaimer.** No interaction offers a prize or requires payment — the underlying behavior was already honest, but the Terms didn't say so formally. Now they do.
4. **Future-proofed narrowly.** Added a short "Future Products and Services" section to each document covering natural media-company extensions — interactive experiences, memberships, digital products, community features, supporting software tools, educational resources, physical products — without inventing features or reading as a speculative technology wishlist. An earlier draft over-reached here (naming unlaunched internal product concepts and buzzwords like "AI-assisted features" in a business-offerings list); this was caught and corrected before finalizing.
5. **Strengthened IP protection for WAG's actual product work** — the software, written rules, distinctive design, and branding of its interactive features and named future concepts (Discovery Platform, Adventure Map, Passport/collection systems) — while deliberately avoiding a claim of ownership over generic, unprotectable ideas like "a quiz" or "a vote," which would weaken the clause rather than strengthen it.
6. **Broadened User Submissions into one consolidated section** covering future content formats (photos, video, artwork, Adventure Map submissions, etc.) instead of repeating the same license grant in multiple places — a readability improvement as much as a coverage one.

## Why It Changed

WAG's content and product surface grew today (interaction platform launch) and is expected to keep growing over the next 5–10 years (memberships, physical products, additional brands). The documents needed to reflect what's real today accurately, and be structured so ordinary future growth doesn't require a ground-up legal rewrite each time.

## Risks Reduced

- **Undisclosed data collection risk** — the interaction platform's votes, quiz answers, free-text submissions, and per-choice email tagging are now disclosed specifically, not just implied by generic "cookies or similar technologies" language.
- **Contest/sweepstakes ambiguity** — now formally disclaimed in the Terms, closing a gap that existed in wording only (the actual product behavior was already compliant).
- **Submission-ownership ambiguity** — Questions Featured and future community-content formats now have an explicit license grant and moderation-rights clause, rather than relying on the general submissions language to cover a use case it wasn't written with in mind.
- **IP dilution risk** — WAG's interactive-feature designs and named future-product concepts are now explicitly claimed as protected work product, not left implicit.

## What Assumptions Remain

- **"Last updated" date and contact email are placeholders** (`[date of publication]`, `[contact email]`) — fill in at time of publication, not before, so the date reflects when the document actually goes live.
- **The documents assume WAG's current entity structure and jurisdiction (United States) are unchanged.** If WAG forms a new entity for a future brand (e.g., a separate legal entity for HorseSmart Kids or a spin-off), that entity would need its own Privacy Policy/Terms or an amendment naming it — not addressed here, since no such entity exists yet.
- **No specific state-law analysis was performed** (e.g., California's CCPA/CPRA, Virginia's VCDPA, or similar state privacy statutes) — the documents use general "applicable law" language throughout rather than citing specific statutes, which is appropriate for a first-pass draft but is exactly the kind of thing counsel should confirm against WAG's actual user geography.
- **No formal COPPA compliance certification was sought** — the Children's Privacy section reflects a considered, defensible posture (not directed at children under 13, no age-gated collection, no under-13-targeted design), but this is WAG's own good-faith analysis, not a legal opinion.

## What Still Deserves Attorney Attention

1. **State-specific privacy-law citations and any required disclosures** (e.g., a formal CCPA "Do Not Sell or Share" mechanism if WAG's data practices trigger that requirement as WAG's audience or ad-tech stack grows).
2. **The Discovery Platform/Adventure Map/Passport IP language** — confirm the "specific expression, not the abstract idea" framing holds up as actually intended and doesn't need tightening for WAG's jurisdiction.
3. **Confirm the informal dispute-resolution and governing-law provisions** are sufficient, or whether WAG wants a formal arbitration clause or venue selection as the company grows (deliberately not added here, since adding one is a real business decision, not a drafting default).
4. **Sign off on the "not a sweepstakes" language** against the specific state sweepstakes/contest statutes relevant to WAG's actual audience states, since requirements vary meaningfully by state even for no-purchase-necessary promotions.

## Recommendation

This package is ready for counsel to **refine, not rebuild**. No red flags were found in the current implementation. The two open items that would benefit most from attorney judgment, not further internal drafting, are #1 and #3 above — both are genuine business/legal-strategy decisions, not accuracy gaps.
